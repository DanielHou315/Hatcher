## Why this paper

Before this paper there were two families of noise-based generative models that looked
unrelated: DDPM (Ho et al. 2020), a discrete Markov chain of Gaussian noising steps trained by
a variational bound, and NCSN (Song and Ermon 2019), a score network trained at several noise
levels and sampled by annealed Langevin dynamics. Song et al. showed both are Euler–Maruyama
discretizations of a forward SDE $\mathrm{d}X_t = f(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_t$,
and that once you have the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ of the noised marginals you get three
things for free: a reverse-time SDE that turns noise into data, a deterministic ODE with the
same marginals, and, from the ODE, an exact log-likelihood. The paper won the ICLR 2021
Outstanding Paper Award and is the reference point every later diffusion, flow-matching, and
diffusion-policy paper measures itself against.

For a robotics reader: Diffusion Policy samples an action chunk by running exactly the
reverse process below with a learned score (written as a noise predictor); $\pi_0$ and other
flow-based policies integrate a vector field that, by the result on the probability flow ODE,
is the score in disguise. When you tune the number of denoising steps at inference time, you
are choosing a discretization of the SDE or ODE of this page.

## Setting and notation

The paper writes $\mathbf{x}(t)$ for the state, $\mathbf{w}$ for Brownian motion, and
$p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}(t)\mid\mathbf{x}(0))$ for the transition kernel. We write $X_t$, $W_t$, and
$p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)$. Throughout, $t\in[0,T]$ with $T = 1$ unless stated, and

$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = f(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_t ,
\qquad X_0 \sim p_0 = p_{\text{data}},
$$

with $f:\mathbb{R}^d\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^d$ and scalar $g:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$.
The marginal density of $X_t$ is $p_t$, and $\nabla \log p_t$ always means the gradient in
$x$: the score. A network $s_\theta(x,t)$ is trained to approximate it.

Standing hypotheses for the whole page, which the paper inherits from Anderson (1982) and
does not restate. We assume $f$ is Lipschitz in $x$ with at most linear growth, $g$ is
continuous, so that the forward SDE has a unique strong solution
([linear SDEs](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou) gives the argument in the linear case). We assume
every $p_t$ is $C^2$ in $x$, strictly positive, and solves the
[Fokker–Planck equation](/concepts/fokker-planck)

$$
\partial_t p_t = -\nabla\cdot\big(f\,p_t\big) + \tfrac12 g(t)^2\,\Delta p_t ,
$$

and that this equation, with the given initial condition, has a unique solution in the class
of probability densities we work with. Finally we assume $\nabla\log p_t$ is locally Lipschitz
with at most linear growth, so the reverse SDE below is well posed. For the linear SDEs of the
paper with Gaussian or compactly supported data, all of this holds for $t>0$; the paper's
Appendix A points at Anderson for the general case.

The three forward SDEs of the paper, with $\beta(t)>0$ and $\sigma(t)$ increasing:

| name | $f(x,t)$ | $g(t)$ |
|---|---|---|
| VE (variance exploding, NCSN) | $0$ | $\sqrt{\mathrm{d}[\sigma^2(t)]/\mathrm{d}t}$ |
| VP (variance preserving, DDPM) | $-\tfrac12\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ |
| sub-VP | $-\tfrac12\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)\big(1-e^{-2\int_0^t\beta}\big)}$ |

## Results at a glance

1. **Reverse-time SDE.** The time reversal of the forward SDE is a diffusion with drift
   $f - g^2\nabla\log p_t$ and the same noise level. Needs
   [fokker-planck](/concepts/fokker-planck),
   [time-reversal-of-diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions).
2. **Probability flow ODE.** $\dot x = f - \tfrac12 g^2\nabla\log p_t(x)$ has marginals $p_t$.
   Needs [continuity-equation](/concepts/continuity-equation),
   [fokker-planck](/concepts/fokker-planck).
3. **Gaussian perturbation kernels.** For VE, VP, and sub-VP, $p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)$ is Gaussian
   with explicit mean and variance. Needs [linear-sde-and-ou](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou),
   [ito-lemma](/concepts/ito-lemma).
4. **Denoising score matching across time.** Regressing $s_\theta(x_t,t)$ onto the
   conditional score $\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)$ recovers the marginal score.
   Needs [score-matching](/concepts/score-matching),
   [conditional-expectation](/concepts/conditional-expectation),
   [gaussian-computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations).
5. **Exact likelihood through the ODE.** $\log p_0(x_0)$ is $\log p_T$ of the ODE endpoint plus
   an integral of the divergence of the ODE velocity, estimated by Hutchinson's trick. Needs
   [continuity-equation](/concepts/continuity-equation),
   [matrix-calculus](/concepts/matrix-calculus).

## Result 1: the reverse-time SDE

<Theorem name="Reverse-time SDE (Anderson 1982, as used by Song et al.)">
Under the standing hypotheses, let $\bar W$ be a Brownian motion in reversed time, i.e.
$\bar W_\tau$ with $\tau = T - t$, independent of $X_T$. The process $Y_\tau := X_{T-\tau}$
solves

$$
\mathrm{d}Y_\tau = \Big[-f(Y_\tau, T-\tau) + g(T-\tau)^2\,\nabla\log p_{T-\tau}(Y_\tau)\Big]\mathrm{d}\tau
+ g(T-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_\tau ,
\qquad Y_0 \sim p_T .
$$

The paper writes the same equation in the original clock $t$, with $\mathrm{d}t$ a negative
increment:
$\mathrm{d}X_t = \big[f(X_t,t) - g(t)^2\nabla\log p_t(X_t)\big]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_t$.
</Theorem>

**Prerequisite check.** You need the Fokker–Planck equation and the identity
$p\,\nabla\log p = \nabla p$ ([fokker-planck](/concepts/fokker-planck)), and it helps to have
seen the same argument for a scalar OU process on
[time-reversal-of-diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions).

<Proof>
We show that the proposed reverse SDE has the right one-time marginals; see the remark after
the proof for what is left to Anderson.

Let $q_\tau$ be the density of the solution $Y_\tau$ of the proposed reverse SDE with
$q_0 = p_T$. Abbreviate $f = f(\cdot,T-\tau)$, $g = g(T-\tau)$, $p = p_{T-\tau}$. The
Fokker–Planck equation of the reverse SDE, read off from its drift $b = -f + g^2\nabla\log p$
and diffusion $g$, is

$$
\partial_\tau q = -\nabla\cdot\big(b\,q\big) + \tfrac12 g^2\Delta q
= \nabla\cdot(f q) - g^2\,\nabla\cdot\big(q\,\nabla\log p\big) + \tfrac12 g^2\Delta q .
$$

We check that $q_\tau := p_{T-\tau}$ solves this. Substituting $q = p$ makes
$q\,\nabla\log p = \nabla p$, hence $\nabla\cdot(q\nabla\log p) = \Delta p$, and the right-hand
side becomes

$$
\nabla\cdot(f p) - g^2\Delta p + \tfrac12 g^2\Delta p
= \nabla\cdot(f p) - \tfrac12 g^2\Delta p
= -\Big[-\nabla\cdot(f p) + \tfrac12 g^2\Delta p\Big]
= -\,\partial_t p_t\big|_{t=T-\tau} .
$$

The last equality is the forward Fokker–Planck equation. On the left-hand side, the chain
rule gives $\partial_\tau\big(p_{T-\tau}\big) = -\partial_t p_t\big|_{t=T-\tau}$. Both sides
agree, so $p_{T-\tau}$ solves the reverse Fokker–Planck equation with initial datum $p_T$.
By the uniqueness hypothesis, $q_\tau = p_{T-\tau}$ for all $\tau\in[0,T]$: the reverse SDE
started from $p_T$ has marginal $p_0 = p_{\text{data}}$ at $\tau = T$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Three things and nothing else: the forward Fokker–Planck
equation, the algebraic identity $p\nabla\log p = \nabla p$, and uniqueness of Fokker–Planck
solutions. The sign flip in front of $f$ is just $\mathrm{d}\tau = -\mathrm{d}t$; the new
score term is what makes the diffusion term of the reverse equation, which has the wrong sign
after time reversal, come out right. Be honest about what this argument does not show: it
proves that the reverse SDE reproduces each marginal $p_t$, which is all a sampler needs.
Anderson's theorem is stronger: the actual time-reversed process $X_{T-\tau}$ is itself a
Markov diffusion with this drift, so path laws agree, not just marginals. That proof uses
the backward Kolmogorov equation for the transition densities and a Bayes-rule computation on
$p(x_s\mid x_t)$ for $s<t$; Haussmann and Pardoux (1986) weakened the smoothness hypotheses.
For a state-dependent matrix diffusion $G(x,t)$, the reverse drift is
$f - \nabla\cdot\big(GG^\top\big) - GG^\top\nabla\log p_t$ (paper, Appendix A); the extra
divergence term vanishes when $G = g(t)I$.

<Warning>
The reverse SDE needs $\nabla\log p_t$ for the marginal $p_t$ of the *forward* process
started at the data, not for the prior. When the sampler is started from $\pi = \mathcal N(0,I)$
instead of $p_T$, the mismatch $\mathrm{KL}(p_T\,\|\,\pi)$ is a bias that no amount of score
accuracy removes; the VP schedule is chosen to make it exponentially small.
</Warning>

## Result 2: the probability flow ODE

<Theorem name="Probability flow ODE">
Under the standing hypotheses, the deterministic flow

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = f(x,t) - \tfrac12 g(t)^2\,\nabla\log p_t(x),
\qquad x(0)\sim p_0 ,
$$

has the same marginal density $p_t$ at every $t$ as the forward SDE. Run backward from
$x(T)\sim p_T$, it produces samples from $p_0$.
</Theorem>

**Prerequisite check.** The [continuity equation](/concepts/continuity-equation): the density
$\rho_t$ of $x(t)$ when $\dot x = v(x,t)$ and $x(0)\sim\rho_0$ solves
$\partial_t\rho = -\nabla\cdot(\rho v)$, uniquely if $v$ is locally Lipschitz with linear
growth.

<Proof>
Rewrite the diffusion term of the forward Fokker–Planck equation as a transport term. Since
$\Delta p = \nabla\cdot(\nabla p) = \nabla\cdot\big(p\,\nabla\log p\big)$,

$$
\partial_t p_t
= -\nabla\cdot(f p_t) + \tfrac12 g^2\,\nabla\cdot\big(p_t\nabla\log p_t\big)
= -\nabla\cdot\Big(\big[f - \tfrac12 g^2\nabla\log p_t\big]\,p_t\Big).
$$

This is the continuity equation with velocity $v(x,t) = f(x,t) - \tfrac12 g(t)^2\nabla\log p_t(x)$.
The flow of $\dot x = v$ started from $p_0$ has a density that solves the same continuity
equation with the same initial condition. Uniqueness gives equality of the two densities for
all $t$. The reverse direction is the same statement read with $\tau = T-t$: an ODE can be
integrated in either direction, and the density transported along it is $p_t$ at every time.
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Only that a Laplacian of a positive density is the divergence
of $p\nabla\log p$. Nothing probabilistic is left: this is why the ODE trajectories are
deterministic given $x(T)$, why they can be integrated with any off-the-shelf ODE solver
(the paper uses an adaptive RK45 solver and needs far fewer network evaluations than its
1000-step SDE sampler), and why
the map $x(T)\mapsto x(0)$ is a bijection, which is what Result 5 exploits. A useful remark
that falls out of the same computation: for any $h(t)\ge 0$, the SDE

$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = \Big[f - \tfrac12\big(g^2 + h^2\big)\nabla\log p_t\Big]\mathrm{d}t + h(t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_t
$$

run in reverse time also has marginals $p_t$; $h = g$ is Result 1, $h = 0$ is Result 2, and
intermediate $h$ trade off stochastic error correction against solver step count. (Check it:
its reverse-time Fokker–Planck equation reduces to the forward one by the same substitution.)

<Intuition>
Diffusion spreads mass out. Along the flow, that spreading is replaced by pushing each
particle down the score, i.e. away from where density is high, with half the strength of the
noise. Half, because a Gaussian kernel of variance $g^2\,\mathrm{d}t$ has second moment
$g^2\,\mathrm{d}t$ but the drift enters the density equation through $\nabla\cdot$, which
picks up $\tfrac12$ from the Itô correction.
</Intuition>

## Result 3: the perturbation kernels are Gaussian

<Proposition name="Perturbation kernels of VE, VP, and sub-VP">
Write $B(t) = \int_0^t\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$. Conditional on $X_0 = x_0$:

- VE: $X_t \sim \mathcal N\big(x_0,\;[\sigma^2(t) - \sigma^2(0)]\,I\big)$.
- VP: $X_t \sim \mathcal N\big(e^{-B(t)/2}x_0,\;[1 - e^{-B(t)}]\,I\big)$.
- sub-VP: $X_t \sim \mathcal N\big(e^{-B(t)/2}x_0,\;[1 - e^{-B(t)}]^2\,I\big)$.
</Proposition>

**Prerequisite check.** The integrating-factor solution of a linear SDE and the Itô
isometry for the variance of a Wiener integral, both on
[linear-sde-and-ou](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou); the OU computation on
[ito-lemma](/concepts/ito-lemma) is the constant-$\beta$ case.

<Proof>
Work in one coordinate; coordinates are independent because $g(t)$ is scalar.

*VE.* There is no drift, so $X_t = x_0 + \int_0^t\sqrt{\tfrac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^2}{\mathrm{d}s}}\,\mathrm{d}W_s$.
A Wiener integral of a deterministic integrand is Gaussian with mean $0$ and, by the Itô
isometry, variance $\int_0^t\tfrac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^2}{\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathrm{d}s = \sigma^2(t) - \sigma^2(0)$.

*VP.* Apply Itô's lemma to $F(x,t) = e^{B(t)/2}x$, with $\partial_t F = \tfrac12\beta e^{B/2}x$,
$\partial_x F = e^{B/2}$, $\partial_{xx}F = 0$:

$$
\mathrm{d}\big(e^{B(t)/2}X_t\big)
= \Big(\tfrac12\beta e^{B/2}X_t - \tfrac12\beta e^{B/2}X_t\Big)\mathrm{d}t + e^{B/2}\sqrt{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}W_t
= \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,e^{B(t)/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_t .
$$

Integrate from $0$ to $t$ and multiply by $e^{-B(t)/2}$:

$$
X_t = e^{-B(t)/2}x_0 + \int_0^t\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,e^{-(B(t)-B(s))/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_s .
$$

The mean is the first term. The variance is, by the Itô isometry and the substitution
$u = B(s)$, $\mathrm{d}u = \beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$,

$$
\int_0^t\beta(s)\,e^{-(B(t)-B(s))}\,\mathrm{d}s
= e^{-B(t)}\int_0^{B(t)}e^{u}\,\mathrm{d}u
= e^{-B(t)}\big(e^{B(t)} - 1\big) = 1 - e^{-B(t)} .
$$

*sub-VP.* The drift is the same, so the mean is the same and the same integrating factor
applies with $g^2(s) = \beta(s)\big(1 - e^{-2B(s)}\big)$. The variance integral is

$$
e^{-B(t)}\int_0^t\beta(s)\big(e^{B(s)} - e^{-B(s)}\big)\mathrm{d}s
= e^{-B(t)}\Big[\big(e^{B(t)}-1\big) + \big(e^{-B(t)}-1\big)\Big]
= 1 - 2e^{-B(t)} + e^{-2B(t)} = \big(1-e^{-B(t)}\big)^2 .
$$

$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Linearity of the drift in $x$ and determinism of $g$; that is
what makes the solution an affine function of $x_0$ plus a Gaussian Wiener integral. The
consequences are the whole training recipe: $p_{0t}$ Gaussian means sampling $x_t$ from
$x_0$ costs one Gaussian draw, and the conditional score in Result 4 is a linear function.
Sanity checks: at $t=0$ all three variances vanish; as $B(t)\to\infty$ the VP and sub-VP
variances tend to $1$, matching the $\mathcal N(0,I)$ prior, and sub-VP's variance is always
smaller than VP's since $(1-e^{-B})^2$ is less than $1-e^{-B}$, which is the point of the name.
DDPM's $\bar\alpha_t = e^{-B(t)}$ with $B$ the integral of its linear $\beta$ schedule.

## Result 4: denoising score matching across time

<Theorem name="Time-weighted denoising score matching">
Let $\lambda:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ be a weighting and $t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]$,
$x_0\sim p_0$, $x_t\sim p_{0t}(\cdot\mid x_0)$. Under the standing hypotheses and
$\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)\|^2 < \infty$ for each $t$, the minimizer over all
measurable $s(x,t)$ with finite second moment of

$$
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DSM}}(s) = \mathbb{E}_t\,\lambda(t)\,\mathbb{E}_{x_0}\mathbb{E}_{x_t\mid x_0}
\Big\|s(x_t,t) - \nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)\Big\|^2
$$

is $s^*(x,t) = \nabla_x\log p_t(x)$ for every $t$ (up to sets of $p_t$-measure zero). For the
Gaussian kernels of Result 3 with mean $\mu_t(x_0)$ and variance $\sigma_t^2 I$,

$$
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0) = -\frac{x_t - \mu_t(x_0)}{\sigma_t^2} .
$$
</Theorem>

**Prerequisite check.** Vincent's denoising score matching identity on
[score-matching](/concepts/score-matching); the $L^2$-projection characterization of
conditional expectation on [conditional-expectation](/concepts/conditional-expectation);
the score of a Gaussian on [gaussian-computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations).

<Proof>
*Closed-form conditional score.* $\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0) = -\|x_t-\mu_t(x_0)\|^2/(2\sigma_t^2) + \text{const}$,
and differentiating in $x_t$ gives the formula. This is available only because Result 3 made
the kernel Gaussian with known parameters; the marginal $p_t$, an integral of this kernel
against the unknown $p_0$, has no closed form.

*Why the conditional target gives the marginal score.* Fix $t$; the weighting $\lambda(t)$
and the average over $t$ do not couple different times, so it suffices to minimize
$\mathbb{E}\|s(x_t) - Y\|^2$ with $Y := \nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)$ over functions of
$x_t$ alone. The minimizer of a squared error over functions of $x_t$ is the conditional
expectation, $s^*(x_t) = \mathbb{E}[Y\mid x_t]$. Compute it using Bayes' rule
$p(x_0\mid x_t) = p_0(x_0)\,p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)/p_t(x_t)$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[Y\mid x_t]
= \int \frac{p_0(x_0)\,p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)}{p_t(x_t)}\cdot\frac{\nabla_{x_t}p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)}{p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)}\,\mathrm{d}x_0
= \frac{1}{p_t(x_t)}\,\nabla_{x_t}\!\int p_0(x_0)\,p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)\,\mathrm{d}x_0
= \frac{\nabla p_t(x_t)}{p_t(x_t)} .
$$

The gradient was moved outside the integral; this is justified by dominated convergence
because the Gaussian kernel and its gradient are bounded uniformly in $x_0$ for $\sigma_t>0$.
The result is $\nabla\log p_t(x_t)$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Two facts: least squares recovers a conditional expectation,
and the conditional expectation of the conditional score is the marginal score, which is
just differentiating under an integral. Vincent's original identity is the same computation
phrased differently: $\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DSM}}$ and the explicit objective
$\mathbb{E}\|s - \nabla\log p_t\|^2$ differ by a constant independent of $s$. The paper's
default weighting is $\lambda(t)\propto 1/\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)\|^2 = \sigma_t^2/d$,
which makes every time contribute an $O(1)$ loss; in noise-prediction form, $s_\theta = -\epsilon_\theta/\sigma_t$,
this is exactly DDPM's unweighted $\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\|^2$. The alternative
$\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ turns $\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DSM}}$ into an upper bound on
$-\mathbb{E}\log p_\theta(x_0)$ (Song, Durkan, Murray, Ermon 2021, <Cite id="2101.09258" />,
derived with [Girsanov](/concepts/girsanov-theorem)), which is where the ELBO view of
[kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds)
reconnects to score matching.

<Warning>
The theorem is about the minimizer over all functions. A finite network trained for finite
time gives $s_\theta\ne\nabla\log p_t$, and the reverse SDE with $s_\theta$ in place of the true
score is a different SDE whose marginals are not $p_t$. The paper's predictor-corrector
sampler (a few Langevin steps at each time using $s_\theta$) is a practical hedge against
this; it is not covered by the results on this page.
</Warning>

## Result 5: exact likelihood through the ODE

<Proposition name="Likelihood via the probability flow ODE">
Let $v_\theta(x,t) = f(x,t) - \tfrac12 g(t)^2 s_\theta(x,t)$ be the learned ODE velocity,
assumed $C^1$ in $x$ with bounded Jacobian on compact sets, and let $x(t)$ solve
$\dot x = v_\theta(x,t)$ with $x(0) = x_0$. Define the model density $p_\theta$ by pulling the
prior $\pi$ at time $T$ back along this flow. Then

$$
\log p_\theta(x_0) = \log\pi\big(x(T)\big) + \int_0^T\nabla\cdot v_\theta\big(x(t),t\big)\,\mathrm{d}t ,
$$

and for any random $\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon\epsilon^\top] = I$,
$\nabla\cdot v_\theta = \operatorname{tr}\big(\partial_x v_\theta\big) = \mathbb{E}_\epsilon\big[\epsilon^\top(\partial_x v_\theta)\,\epsilon\big]$.
</Proposition>

**Prerequisite check.** The instantaneous change of variables on
[continuity-equation](/concepts/continuity-equation) and the trace and Jacobian rules on
[matrix-calculus](/concepts/matrix-calculus).

<Proof>
Let $\rho_t$ be the density transported by $v_\theta$ from $\rho_0 = p_\theta$, so
$\partial_t\rho = -\nabla\cdot(\rho v_\theta)$ and $\rho_T = \pi$. Differentiate
$\log\rho_t(x(t))$ along a trajectory with the chain rule and expand the divergence of a
product:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\rho_t(x(t))
= \frac{\partial_t\rho}{\rho} + \langle\nabla\log\rho, v_\theta\rangle
= \frac{-\rho\,\nabla\cdot v_\theta - \langle\nabla\rho, v_\theta\rangle}{\rho} + \langle\nabla\log\rho, v_\theta\rangle
= -\nabla\cdot v_\theta .
$$

Integrate from $0$ to $T$ and rearrange: $\log\rho_0(x_0) = \log\rho_T(x(T)) + \int_0^T\nabla\cdot v_\theta\,\mathrm{d}t$.
For the trace identity, $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon^\top J\epsilon] = \mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(J\epsilon\epsilon^\top) = \operatorname{tr}(J\,\mathbb{E}[\epsilon\epsilon^\top]) = \operatorname{tr}J$.
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Only the continuity equation, so the formula is exact for the
*model* $p_\theta$ (the ODE with $s_\theta$ is a continuous normalizing flow), whatever the score
error; the approximation is in $p_\theta\ne p_0$, not in the likelihood computation. The
divergence of a $d$-dimensional network costs $d$ backward passes exactly; Hutchinson's
estimator costs one vector-Jacobian product per ODE step, at the price of an unbiased but
noisy divergence, and the paper reports $2.99$ bits/dim on CIFAR-10 this way. In practice the
ODE is integrated from $x_0$ forward to $x(T)$ with the divergence accumulated as an extra
state variable.

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="VP SDE with constant beta on Gaussian data: p_t, score, reverse SDE and ODE">
Take $d = 1$, $p_0 = \mathcal N(2, 0.25)$ (standard deviation $0.5$), and the VP SDE with
constant $\beta = 2$ on $t\in[0,1]$:

$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = -X_t\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}W_t ,
\qquad f(x,t) = -x,\quad g(t) = \sqrt 2 .
$$

*Marginal.* By Result 3 with $B(t) = 2t$, $X_t\mid X_0 = x_0\sim\mathcal N(e^{-t}x_0,\;1-e^{-2t})$.
Since $X_0$ is Gaussian and independent of $W$, $X_t$ is Gaussian with

$$
m_t = 2e^{-t},
\qquad
v_t = e^{-2t}\cdot 0.25 + \big(1-e^{-2t}\big) = 1 - 0.75\,e^{-2t} .
$$

Check: $v_0 = 0.25$ and $v_t\to 1$, $m_t\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$, the $\mathcal N(0,1)$ prior.
Check against Fokker–Planck, which for a Gaussian reduces to $\dot m = -m$ and
$\dot v = -2v + 2$: indeed $\dot v_t = 1.5\,e^{-2t} = 2(1 - v_t)$.

*Score.* $\nabla\log p_t(x) = -(x - m_t)/v_t$.

*Reverse SDE drift.* $f - g^2\nabla\log p_t = -x + 2\,(x - m_t)/v_t$.

*Numbers at $t = 1$.* $m_1 = 2e^{-1} = 0.735759$, $e^{-2} = 0.135335$,
$v_1 = 1 - 0.101501 = 0.898499$. At $x_1 = 0.7$:

$$
\nabla\log p_1(0.7) = -\frac{0.7 - 0.735759}{0.898499} = \frac{0.035759}{0.898499} = 0.039798 ,
$$

$$
\text{reverse drift} = -0.7 + 2\times 0.039798 = -0.620403 .
$$

*One Euler–Maruyama step of the reverse SDE from $t=1$ to $t=0.9$.* Step size $h = 0.1$
backward, noise sample $\xi = -0.3$. The update is
$x_{t-h} = x_t - \big[f - g^2\nabla\log p_t\big](x_t)\,h + g\sqrt h\,\xi$:

$$
x_{0.9} = 0.7 - (-0.620403)(0.1) + \sqrt 2\cdot\sqrt{0.1}\cdot(-0.3)
= 0.7 + 0.062040 - 0.447214\times 0.3
= 0.7 + 0.062040 - 0.134164 = 0.627876 .
$$

*One Euler step of the probability flow ODE from the same point.* The velocity is
$f - \tfrac12 g^2\nabla\log p_t = -x - \nabla\log p_t(x)$, so at $(0.7, 1)$ it is
$-0.7 - 0.039798 = -0.739798$, and

$$
x_{0.9} = 0.7 - (-0.739798)(0.1) = 0.7 + 0.073980 = 0.773980 .
$$

*Compare.* The ODE moves the point deterministically toward the mean of $p_{0.9}$, which is
$m_{0.9} = 2e^{-0.9} = 0.813139$; the SDE step has the same drift-type motion plus a noise
kick of size $\sqrt{0.2}\,\xi$, which here happened to push it the other way. Two sanity
checks on the numbers:

1. *The ODE step against the exact flow.* For Gaussian marginals the probability flow ODE
   is linear, $\dot x = -x + (x - m_t)/v_t$, and preserves the standardized coordinate
   $z = (x - m_t)/\sqrt{v_t}$ (differentiate $m_t + z\sqrt{v_t}$ and use $\dot m = -m$,
   $\dot v = 2(1-v)$). Here $z = -0.035759/0.947892 = -0.037725$ and
   $\sqrt{v_{0.9}} = \sqrt{0.876026} = 0.935963$, so the exact ODE value is
   $0.813139 - 0.037725\times 0.935963 = 0.777830$. Euler gave $0.773980$; the gap of
   $0.004$ is the expected $O(h^2)$ local error.
2. *The SDE step in expectation.* Averaging the Euler–Maruyama update over $x_1\sim p_1$ and
   $\xi$ gives $\mathbb{E}[x_{0.9}] = m_1 + h\,m_1 - 2h\,\mathbb{E}[\nabla\log p_1(X_1)] = 1.1\times 0.735759 = 0.809335$,
   using that the expected score is zero (it is $\int\nabla p_1 = 0$). The true mean is
   $m_{0.9} = 0.813139$; again the mismatch is $O(h^2)$.
</Example>

## Reading order

1. [ito-lemma](/concepts/ito-lemma): the chain rule that produces the integrating factor
   in Result 3 and the $\tfrac12 g^2$ in every Fokker–Planck equation.
2. [linear-sde-and-ou](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou): solve the VP SDE, get the OU mean
   and variance ODEs, and see DDPM as Euler–Maruyama.
3. [fokker-planck](/concepts/fokker-planck): the density equation and the
   $p\nabla\log p = \nabla p$ rewriting used in Results 1 and 2.
4. [continuity-equation](/concepts/continuity-equation): transport by a velocity field and
   the instantaneous change of variables used in Results 2 and 5.
5. [time-reversal-of-diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions): Result 1 and 2 for
   an OU process, one dimension, everything explicit.
6. [score-matching](/concepts/score-matching): explicit and denoising score matching,
   Vincent's identity, Tweedie; Result 4 is this at all times at once.
7. [diffusion-sde-framework](/concepts/diffusion-sde-framework): the same material as this
   page in lecture form, plus predictor-corrector sampling.

After this paper, [flow-matching-lipman-2022](/papers/flow-matching-lipman-2022) shows how
to regress the probability flow velocity directly, and
[diffusion-schrodinger-bridge-2021](/papers/diffusion-schrodinger-bridge-2021) replaces the
fixed forward SDE with one learned to hit the prior in finite time.
