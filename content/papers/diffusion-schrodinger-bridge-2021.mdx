## Why this paper

Score-based generative models <Cite id="2011.13456" /> only work because the forward noising
process has run long enough that its terminal law is almost Gaussian; the reverse process then
starts from the Gaussian and undoes the noise. DSB removes the "long enough". It asks for the
diffusion closest (in KL) to a reference process whose time-$0$ law is $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and
whose time-$T$ law is $p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ *exactly*: the Schrödinger bridge. The paper shows that
the classical iterative proportional fitting (IPF) algorithm for this problem is a sequence of
time reversals of diffusions, each of which is learnable by the same kind of regression as
denoising score matching. That observation turned the Schrödinger bridge from a topic in
stochastic control into a trainable generative model, and it is the ancestor of bridge matching,
DSBM, and $\mathrm{I}^2\mathrm{SB}$. For a robotics reader, the point is that neither endpoint has
to be Gaussian: one can bridge a prior policy's action distribution to an expert's, or one
sensor modality to another, with a handful of integration steps.

The paper also contains the first convergence theorem for score-based generative models (its
Theorem 1, a total-variation bound in terms of score error, step size, and horizon). We do not
decompose it here; it belongs with the [diffusion SDE page](/concepts/diffusion-sde-framework).

## Setting and notation

The paper works in discrete time first and passes to continuous time afterwards. We translate
into the site's conventions: Brownian motion is $W_t$ (the paper writes $B_t$), and the
paper's noise scale $\sqrt{2}$ is kept, so $g^2 = 2$ throughout.

**Discrete time.** A path is $x_{0:N} = (x_0,\dots,x_N)\in(\mathbb{R}^d)^{N+1}$. The
*reference* is a Markov chain with density

$$
p(x_{0:N}) = p_0(x_0)\prod_{k=0}^{N-1} p_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k),
\qquad
p_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k) = \mathcal N\big(x_{k+1};\, x_k + \gamma_{k+1} f(x_k),\, 2\gamma_{k+1} I\big),
$$

with $p_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and step sizes $\gamma_{k+1} > 0$. This is Euler–Maruyama for
$\mathrm{d}X_t = f(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ with $T = \sum_k \gamma_k$. We
write $\mathcal P_{N+1}$ for probability measures on paths, $\pi_k$ for the marginal of $\pi$ at
step $k$, $\pi_{0,N}$ for the joint law of $(x_0,x_N)$, and $\pi_{|0,N}$ for the conditional law
of the interior $x_{1:N-1}$ given both endpoints. Every chain also has a backward factorization
$p(x_{0:N}) = p_N(x_N)\prod_k p_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1})$ with
$p_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1}) = p_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k)\,p_k(x_k)/p_{k+1}(x_{k+1})$ (Bayes).

**Continuous time.** $\mathbb P$ is the path measure of the reference SDE on $[0,T]$, $\Pi$ a
candidate path measure, $\Pi_t$ its marginal at time $t$. KL between path measures is defined
through Girsanov, see [Girsanov's theorem](/concepts/girsanov-theorem).

**The problem.** The dynamic Schrödinger bridge (SB) is

$$
\pi^\star = \operatorname*{arg\,min}\big\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi\,|\,p) : \pi\in\mathcal P_{N+1},\ \pi_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}},\ \pi_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}\big\}.
$$

Two facts from [KL divergence](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds) are used over
and over. The *chain rule*: if $\pi\ll p$ and we split a path into a coordinate $y$ and the rest
$z$, then $\operatorname{KL}(\pi|p) = \operatorname{KL}(\pi_y|p_y) + \mathbb{E}_{\pi_y}\big[\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot\mid y)\,|\,p(\cdot\mid y))\big]$.
And *strict convexity* of $\pi\mapsto\operatorname{KL}(\pi|p)$, which gives uniqueness of minimizers
over convex constraint sets.

## Result 1: the bridge is a KL projection, and it reduces to a static problem

<Theorem name="Static reduction of the Schrödinger bridge">
Assume some path measure $\tilde\pi$ with $\tilde\pi_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$, $\tilde\pi_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ has $\operatorname{KL}(\tilde\pi|p)$ finite (the paper's sufficient condition is $\operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes p_{\mathrm{prior}}\,|\,p_{0,N})$ finite). Then:

1. The dynamic SB $\pi^\star$ exists and is unique, and
   $\pi^\star(x_{0:N}) = \pi^{s,\star}(x_0,x_N)\,p_{|0,N}(x_{1:N-1}\mid x_0,x_N)$: between the endpoints it is the *reference's* bridge.
2. The endpoint coupling solves the static problem
   $\pi^{s,\star} = \operatorname{arg\,min}\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi^s\,|\,p_{0,N}) : \pi^s_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}},\ \pi^s_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}\}$.
3. If the reference is driftless with $p_{N|0}(x_N\mid x_0) = \mathcal N(x_N; x_0, \sigma^2 I)$, the static problem is entropic OT with quadratic cost:
   $\pi^{s,\star} = \operatorname{arg\,min}\{\mathbb{E}_{\pi^s}\|X_0 - X_N\|^2 - 2\sigma^2 H(\pi^s)\}$ over the same couplings, where $H$ is differential entropy.
</Theorem>

**Prerequisite check.** Chain rule of KL and strict convexity
([kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds)); the
definition of entropic OT ([entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn](/concepts/entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn)); the
static/dynamic vocabulary of the [Schrödinger bridge page](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge).

<Proof>
*Reduction (1 and 2).* Apply the chain rule with $y = (x_0,x_N)$ and $z = x_{1:N-1}$:

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi|p) = \operatorname{KL}(\pi_{0,N}\,|\,p_{0,N}) + \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0,N}}\Big[\operatorname{KL}\big(\pi_{|0,N}\,\big|\,p_{|0,N}\big)\Big].
$$

The constraints only touch $\pi_{0,N}$. The second term is a nonnegative expectation and can be
set to zero, without changing $\pi_{0,N}$, by choosing $\pi_{|0,N} = p_{|0,N}$. So any minimizer
has the reference bridge inside, and its endpoint coupling minimizes the first term: that is
statement 2. Uniqueness is strict convexity of KL on the convex set of feasible couplings.
Existence is the one step we do not prove: it uses that sublevel sets of
$\pi\mapsto\operatorname{KL}(\pi|p)$ are weakly compact and that the constraint set is weakly closed
(Léonard 2014, Section 2); the finiteness assumption guarantees the infimum is finite.

*Entropic OT form (3).* With $p_{0,N}(x_0,x_N) = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_0)\,p_{N|0}(x_N\mid x_0)$,

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi^s|p_{0,N})
= \int \pi^s\log\pi^s - \int\pi^s\log p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_0) - \int \pi^s \log p_{N|0}(x_N\mid x_0)
= -H(\pi^s) + H(p_{\mathrm{data}}) - \mathbb{E}_{\pi^s}\big[\log p_{N|0}(X_N\mid X_0)\big].
$$

The middle term is a constant because $\pi^s_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$ is fixed. For the Gaussian
kernel, $-\log p_{N|0}(x_N\mid x_0) = \|x_N - x_0\|^2/(2\sigma^2) + \mathrm{const}$, so the
objective is $\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\mathbb{E}\|X_0-X_N\|^2 - H(\pi^s)$ up to constants; multiply by
$2\sigma^2$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**Continuous time.** The same chain rule holds for path measures with the endpoints
$(x_0, x_T)$ as $y$, and the reference bridge $\mathbb P_{|0,T}$ is the reference SDE
conditioned on both endpoints (for Brownian motion, the Brownian bridge). Girsanov turns the
objective into a control cost: if $\Pi$ is the law of
$\mathrm{d}X_t = u_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}W_t$, then

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\Pi|\mathbb P) = \operatorname{KL}(\Pi_0|\mathbb P_0) + \tfrac14\,\mathbb{E}_\Pi\int_0^T \|u_t(X_t) - f(X_t)\|^2\,\mathrm{d}t ,
$$

so the SB is the minimum-energy drift correction that hits both marginals. The $\tfrac14$ is
$1/(2g^2)$ with $g^2 = 2$.

**What the proof really uses.** Only the chain rule of KL and the fact that the constraints are
functions of the endpoint marginals. Nothing about Gaussians, diffusions, or dimension enters
until statement 3. That is why the same reduction is used for every reference process in the
bridge-matching literature.

## Result 2: IPF is alternating half-bridges, which is Sinkhorn in path space

<Definition name="Iterative proportional fitting">
Start from $\pi^0 = p$ and alternate KL projections onto the two marginal constraints:

$$
\pi^{2n+1} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n}) : \pi_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}\},
\qquad
\pi^{2n+2} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n+1}) : \pi_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}\}.
$$

Each projection is a *half-bridge*: one marginal is imposed, the other is left free.
</Definition>

<Proposition name="Half-bridges keep one direction's transitions (the paper's representation of the IPF iterates)">
Under $\operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes p_{\mathrm{prior}}\,|\,p_{0,N})$ finite, every iterate has a positive density, $\pi^{2n} = p^n$, $\pi^{2n+1} = q^n$, with $p^0 = p$ and

$$
q^n(x_{0:N}) = p_{\mathrm{prior}}(x_N)\prod_{k=0}^{N-1} p^n_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1}),
\qquad
p^{n+1}(x_{0:N}) = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_0)\prod_{k=0}^{N-1} q^n_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k).
$$

In words: the odd iterate is the *backward* chain of the even one, re-started from $p_{\mathrm{prior}}$; the even iterate is the *forward* chain of the odd one, re-started from $p_{\mathrm{data}}$.
</Proposition>

**Prerequisite check.** Chain rule of KL again; Bayes for reverse transitions; the Sinkhorn
update rule from [entropic OT](/concepts/entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn).

<Proof>
Take the odd step. Chain rule with $y = x_N$, $z = x_{0:N-1}$:

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n}) = \operatorname{KL}(\pi_N\,|\,\pi^{2n}_N) + \mathbb{E}_{\pi_N}\Big[\operatorname{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x_N)\,\big|\,\pi^{2n}(\cdot\mid x_N)\big)\Big].
$$

The constraint $\pi_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ pins the first term at
$\operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{prior}}|\pi^{2n}_N)$, which does not depend on $\pi$. The second term is
minimized, uniquely, by $\pi(\cdot\mid x_N) = \pi^{2n}(\cdot\mid x_N)$. So
$\pi^{2n+1}(x_{0:N}) = p_{\mathrm{prior}}(x_N)\,\pi^{2n}(x_{0:N-1}\mid x_N)$, and since $\pi^{2n}$
is Markov, its conditional given $x_N$ factorizes into the product of backward transitions
$p^n_{k|k+1}$. The even step is the mirror image with $y = x_0$. Positivity of densities is
inherited from $p>0$ by induction; the finiteness assumption is what makes each
$\operatorname{arg\,min}$ exist (Result 4 shows the objective stays finite along the sequence).
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**Why this is Sinkhorn.** Look at endpoints only. The odd step multiplies the joint by the ratio
of the imposed to the current terminal marginal:

$$
\pi^{2n+1}_{0,N}(x,y) = \pi^{2n}_{0,N}(x,y)\,\frac{p_{\mathrm{prior}}(y)}{\pi^{2n}_N(y)} ,
\qquad
\pi^{2n+2}_{0,N}(x,y) = \pi^{2n+1}_{0,N}(x,y)\,\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{\pi^{2n+1}_0(x)} .
$$

By induction $\pi^{2n}_{0,N}(x,y) = a_n(x)\,p_{0,N}(x,y)\,b_{n-1}(y)$: a column rescaling, then a
row rescaling, of the fixed kernel $p_{0,N}$, which is exactly the Sinkhorn iteration
$u\odot K\odot v$ from the [entropic OT page](/concepts/entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn) with
$K = p_{0,N}$, in continuous state space. And the interior of the path is never touched: if
$\pi^{2n} = \pi^{2n}_{0,N}\otimes p_{|0,N}$, then
$\pi^{2n+1} = p_{\mathrm{prior}}(x_N)\,\pi^{2n}(x_0\mid x_N)\,p_{|0,N}(x_{1:N-1}\mid x_0,x_N)$
still has the reference bridge inside. So IPF on path space *is* Sinkhorn on the endpoint
coupling, glued to the reference bridge, which is the paper's statement
$\Pi^n = \pi^{s,n}\,\mathbb P_{|0,T}$ in continuous time.

**What the proof really uses.** The chain rule, with the split chosen so that the constraint
sits entirely in the first term. The Markov property is used only to write the conditional as a
product of one-step transitions, which is what makes the half-bridge *simulable*.

## Result 3: half-bridges are time reversals, and reversing a Gaussian step is a regression

The paper's contribution is to notice that "the backward chain of $p^n$ re-started from
$p_{\mathrm{prior}}$" is a thing one can *learn*, because backward transitions of a diffusion are
given by Anderson's formula.

<Proposition name="Continuous-time IPF iterates are diffusions (paraphrase of the paper's continuous-time proposition)">
Suppose $\Pi^{2n}$ is the law of $\mathrm{d}X_t = f^n_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}W_t$, $X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$, with marginal densities $p^n_t$, and that the regularity needed for Anderson's theorem holds (the paper assumes a dominating reference diffusion with a confining potential and finite KL of every iterate to it). Then:

- the time reversal of $\Pi^{2n+1}$ is the law of $\mathrm{d}Y_s = b^n_{T-s}(Y_s)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}W_s$, $Y_0\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}$, with
  $b^n_t = -f^n_t + 2\nabla\log p^n_t$;
- $\Pi^{2n+2}$ is the law of $\mathrm{d}X_t = f^{n+1}_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}W_t$, $X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$, with $f^{n+1}_t = -b^n_t + 2\nabla\log q^n_t$, where $q^n_t$ is the marginal of $\Pi^{2n+1}$ at time $t$.
</Proposition>

**Prerequisite check.** Anderson's reverse-time SDE
([time-reversal-of-diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions)): for
$\mathrm{d}X_t = f\,\mathrm{d}t + g\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ with marginals $p_t$, the process
$Y_s = X_{T-s}$ solves $\mathrm{d}Y_s = \big(-f + g^2\nabla\log p_{T-s}\big)(Y_s)\,\mathrm{d}s + g\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_s$.
With $g^2 = 2$ this is the $b^n$ above.

<Proof>
By Result 2, $\Pi^{2n+1}$ has the backward transitions of $\Pi^{2n}$ and initial law
$p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ at time $T$. A Markov process is determined by its transitions and its initial
law, and Anderson's theorem says the backward transitions of $\Pi^{2n}$ are those of the SDE
with drift $-f^n + 2\nabla\log p^n$. Re-starting that SDE from $p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ instead of
$p^n_T$ changes nothing about the transitions. The even step is the same argument applied to the
reversed process, whose marginals are $q^n_t$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**Discrete time, and where the regression comes from.** With Euler steps the reverse transition
is not exactly Gaussian, but it is close. From Bayes,

$$
p^n_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1}) = p^n_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k)\exp\big[\log p^n_k(x_k) - \log p^n_{k+1}(x_{k+1})\big].
$$

Write $x_k = x_{k+1} + \delta$, Taylor-expand $\log p^n_k$ around $x_{k+1}$, replace
$p^n_k$ by $p^n_{k+1}$ and $f(x_k)$ by $f(x_{k+1})$ (each an $O(\gamma)$ change), and complete
the square in $\delta$: the exponent becomes $-\|\delta + \gamma f(x_{k+1}) - 2\gamma\nabla\log p^n_{k+1}(x_{k+1})\|^2/(4\gamma)$
plus terms of higher order in $\gamma$. This is the paper's approximation

$$
p^n_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1}) \approx \mathcal N\big(x_k;\ x_{k+1} + \gamma_{k+1} b^n_{k+1}(x_{k+1}),\ 2\gamma_{k+1}I\big),
\qquad b^n_{k+1} = -f^n_k + 2\nabla\log p^n_{k+1},
$$

the discrete shadow of Anderson. One could now estimate $\nabla\log p^n_{k+1}$ by score matching,
but that would require keeping every past score network, since $f^{n+1}$ is defined through
$b^n$, which is defined through $f^n$, and so on. The paper's alternative is the following identity.

<Proposition name="Mean-matching characterization of the reverse step (the paper's regression proposition)">
Suppose the forward and backward steps at IPF stage $n$ have the Gaussian forms
$p^n_{k+1|k}(x_{k+1}\mid x_k) = \mathcal N(x_{k+1}; F^n_k(x_k), 2\gamma_{k+1}I)$ and
$q^n_{k|k+1}(x_k\mid x_{k+1}) = \mathcal N(x_k; B^n_{k+1}(x_{k+1}), 2\gamma_{k+1}I)$ with
$F^n_k(x) = x + \gamma_{k+1}f^n_k(x)$, $B^n_{k+1}(x) = x + \gamma_{k+1}b^n_{k+1}(x)$ and
$b^n_{k+1} = -f^n_k + 2\nabla\log p^n_{k+1}$. Then

$$
B^n_{k+1} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{B\in L^2}\ \mathbb{E}_{p^n_{k,k+1}}\Big[\big\|B(X_{k+1}) - \big(X_{k+1} + F^n_k(X_k) - F^n_k(X_{k+1})\big)\big\|^2\Big],
$$

and symmetrically $F^{n+1}_k = \operatorname{arg\,min}_F \mathbb{E}_{q^n_{k,k+1}}\big[\|F(X_k) - (X_k + B^n_{k+1}(X_{k+1}) - B^n_{k+1}(X_k))\|^2\big]$.
</Proposition>

**Prerequisite check.** Tweedie's formula from [score matching](/concepts/score-matching): if
$X' = m(X) + \sqrt{2\gamma}\,Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ independent of $X$, then
$2\gamma\,\nabla\log p_{X'}(x') = \mathbb{E}[m(X) - x'\mid X' = x']$. And the fact that the
$L^2$-optimal predictor of a target given $X'$ is the conditional expectation
([conditional expectation](/concepts/conditional-expectation)).

<Proof>
Under $p^n$, $X_{k+1} = F^n_k(X_k) + \sqrt{2\gamma_{k+1}}\,Z$, so
$p^n_{k+1}(x') = (4\pi\gamma)^{-d/2}\int p^n_k(x)\exp[-\|F^n_k(x) - x'\|^2/(4\gamma)]\,\mathrm{d}x$.
Differentiate under the integral (dominated convergence, using $p^n_k$ bounded) and divide by
$p^n_{k+1}(x')$:

$$
\nabla\log p^n_{k+1}(x') = \int \frac{F^n_k(x) - x'}{2\gamma}\,p^n_{k|k+1}(x\mid x')\,\mathrm{d}x
= \mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{F^n_k(X_k) - X_{k+1}}{2\gamma}\ \Big|\ X_{k+1} = x'\Big],
$$

which is Tweedie. Substitute into $b^n_{k+1} = -f^n_k + 2\nabla\log p^n_{k+1}$:

$$
\gamma\,b^n_{k+1}(x') = -\gamma f^n_k(x') + \mathbb{E}[F^n_k(X_k) - x'\mid X_{k+1} = x']
= \mathbb{E}\big[F^n_k(X_k) - F^n_k(x')\ \big|\ X_{k+1} = x'\big],
$$

using $F^n_k(x') = x' + \gamma f^n_k(x')$. Adding $x'$ to both sides,
$B^n_{k+1}(x') = \mathbb{E}[X_{k+1} + F^n_k(X_k) - F^n_k(X_{k+1})\mid X_{k+1} = x']$, and a
conditional expectation is the $L^2$ minimizer of the squared error to its target. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Warning>
The target is $X_{k+1} + F^n_k(X_k) - F^n_k(X_{k+1})$, not the naive $X_k$. The two differ by
$\gamma_{k+1}\big(f^n_k(X_k) - f^n_k(X_{k+1})\big) = O(\gamma^{3/2})$ since
$\|X_k - X_{k+1}\| = O(\gamma^{1/2})$. Regressing on $X_k$ would give the exact Gaussian
projection of the true reverse kernel; the paper's target is the one consistent with the
Anderson-type approximation above, and it is what Algorithm 1 in the paper trains. In
practice the network is parametrized as $B_\beta(k,x)$ and trained on samples of the forward
chain; then $F_\alpha(k,x)$ is trained on samples of the backward chain; and so on.
</Warning>

**What the proof really uses.** Two things only: that the forward step is Gaussian with a known
mean map, which is what makes Tweedie apply, and that the reverse drift is
"$-f$ plus twice the score". No optimality of the bridge is used. This is why the same trick
works for any pair of forward/backward Gaussian chains, and why DSB can be run with a learned
drift as reference.

## Result 4: convergence of IPF

<Theorem name="IPF converges (paraphrase of the paper's two convergence propositions)">
Assume (the paper's A1): $p_N > 0$, $p_{\mathrm{prior}} > 0$, $|H(p_{\mathrm{prior}})|$ finite, and
$\int |\log p_{N|0}(x_N\mid x_0)|\,p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_0)\,p_{\mathrm{prior}}(x_N)\,\mathrm{d}x_0\,\mathrm{d}x_N$ finite. Then:

1. The IPF sequence is well defined, and it is monotone: for $n\ge1$,
   $\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n+1}|\pi^n)\le\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n-1}|\pi^n)$, and $\|\pi^{n+1}-\pi^n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ is non-increasing.
2. The marginal errors vanish faster than $1/n$:
   $n\,\big\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi^n_0|p_{\mathrm{data}}) + \operatorname{KL}(\pi^n_N|p_{\mathrm{prior}})\big\}\to0$.
3. There is a probability measure $\pi^\infty$ with $\|\pi^n - \pi^\infty\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0$.
4. If in addition $h = p_{0,N}/(p_0\otimes p_N)$ is continuous and positive, bounded by a product $\Phi_0(x_0)\Phi_N(x_N)$ of continuous positive functions with $\log h,\log\Phi_0,\log\Phi_N$ integrable against $p_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes p_{\mathrm{prior}}$, and $p\ll\pi^\infty$, then $\pi^\infty = \pi^\star$.
</Theorem>

The paper's discussion is careful to say that earlier results (Rüschendorf 1995) needed $h$
bounded below, and that quantitative rates in the non-compact case were essentially absent; the
$o(1/n)$ is new. Geometric rates hold in the Gaussian case (the paper's Appendix G), and we see
one by hand below.

**Prerequisite check.** Chain rule and Pinsker's inequality
([kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds));
the potential form $a(x)\,p_{0,N}(x,y)\,b(y)$ of the bridge ([schrodinger-bridge](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge)).

<Proof>
We prove the two identities that drive everything and then say what they do and do not give.

*Pythagoras for a marginal projection.* Let $\pi^{2n+1}$ be the projection of $\pi^{2n}$ onto
$\mathcal C_N = \{\pi : \pi_N = p_{\mathrm{prior}}\}$, and let $\pi\in\mathcal C_N$ be any measure with
$\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n})$ finite. Then

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n}) = \operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n+1}) + \operatorname{KL}(\pi^{2n+1}|\pi^{2n}),
$$

with equality, not just inequality. Indeed, by the chain rule with $y = x_N$ and Result 2:
$\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n}) = \operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{prior}}|\pi^{2n}_N) + \mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{prior}}}[\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x_N)|\pi^{2n}(\cdot|x_N))]$;
$\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{2n+1}) = 0 + \mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{prior}}}[\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x_N)|\pi^{2n}(\cdot|x_N))]$ because $\pi^{2n+1}$ has the same conditionals as $\pi^{2n}$ and terminal law $p_{\mathrm{prior}}$;
and $\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{2n+1}|\pi^{2n}) = \operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{prior}}|\pi^{2n}_N) + 0$. Add up. The
same holds for even steps with $\mathcal C_0$.

*Csiszár's telescoping identity.* Let $\tilde\pi$ satisfy both constraints with
$\operatorname{KL}(\tilde\pi|p)$ finite (the product coupling glued to the reference bridge works, by
the assumption). Apply Pythagoras at every step and sum:

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\tilde\pi|\pi^0) = \operatorname{KL}(\tilde\pi|\pi^n) + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{k+1}|\pi^k)
\qquad\text{for all } n.
$$

Consequences: every $\operatorname{KL}(\tilde\pi|\pi^n)$ is finite, so the next projection exists
(this is item 1's "well defined"); the increments $\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{k+1}|\pi^k)$ are summable;
and since $\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{2n+1}|\pi^{2n}) = \operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{prior}}|\pi^{2n}_N)$ and
$\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{2n+2}|\pi^{2n+1}) = \operatorname{KL}(p_{\mathrm{data}}|\pi^{2n+1}_0)$, the marginal
errors (in this direction of KL) are summable.

*From summable to $o(1/n)$.* The paper shows the increments are also non-increasing (item 1;
its proof is another application of the chain rule to the two-step composition, which we skip).
A non-increasing summable sequence $c_n$ satisfies $n c_n\to0$: for $n\ge 2n_0$,
$(n/2)\,c_n\le\sum_{k\ge n_0}c_k$, which is small. That is item 2, after using the chain rule
once more to swap the direction of the marginal KL.

*What we do not reproduce.* Item 3, convergence of the whole sequence in TV, is the delicate
step. Pinsker gives $\|\pi^{k+1}-\pi^k\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{k+1}|\pi^k)/2}$, and a
square root of a summable sequence is not automatically summable, so one needs more than the
identities above; see the paper's Appendix F and Rüschendorf 1995. Item 4, identifying the
limit with $\pi^\star$, uses that each iterate has the product form
$a_n(x)\,p_{0,N}(x,y)\,b_n(y)$ (Result 2), passes the products $a_n b_n$ to the limit along the
TV-convergent sequence, and shows the limit is again a product $a(x)b(y)$ times $p_{0,N}$ with
the right marginals, which characterizes the bridge (the Schrödinger system). The extra
condition on $h$ is what controls the limit of the potentials on a set of full measure.
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**What the proof really uses.** Pythagoras for KL projections onto sets defined by a marginal
constraint, which is exact because the constraint sits in one term of the chain rule. Sinkhorn
convergence proofs are the same argument in discrete state space. Nothing about diffusions is
used, which is why the result transfers to continuous time verbatim through
$\Pi^n = \pi^{s,n}\,\mathbb P_{|0,T}$.

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="The Schrödinger bridge between two 1-D Gaussians under Brownian reference">
**Data.** Reference: Brownian motion with unit diffusion on $[0,1]$, so
$p_{1|0}(y\mid x) = \mathcal N(y; x, 1)$. Marginals: $p_0 = \mathcal N(0,1)$, $p_1 = \mathcal N(3,1)$.
The reference initial law does not matter (it only shifts the objective by a constant, as in
Result 1), so take it to be $p_0$; then the reference endpoint joint is
$R = \mathcal N\big((0,0), \Sigma_R\big)$ with $\Sigma_R = \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$,
$\det\Sigma_R = 1$, $\Sigma_R^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}$.

**Step 1: the static problem.** By Result 1 we minimize $\operatorname{KL}(\pi|R)$ over couplings of
$\mathcal N(0,1)$ and $\mathcal N(3,1)$. Among couplings with the same second moments, the Gaussian
has the largest entropy, and by Result 1(3) the objective is
$\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(Y-X)^2] - H(\pi) + \mathrm{const}$, which depends on $\pi$ only through its
second moments and its entropy. So the minimizer is Gaussian,
$\pi_\rho = \mathcal N\big((0,3), \Sigma_\rho\big)$, $\Sigma_\rho = \begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{pmatrix}$,
and one scalar $\rho\in(-1,1)$ remains. Use the Gaussian KL formula from
[Gaussian computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations):

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi_\rho|R) = \tfrac12\Big[\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_R^{-1}\Sigma_\rho) + \mu^\top\Sigma_R^{-1}\mu - 2 + \log\tfrac{\det\Sigma_R}{\det\Sigma_\rho}\Big],
\qquad \mu = (0,3)^\top .
$$

Compute each piece: $\Sigma_R^{-1}\Sigma_\rho = \begin{pmatrix}2-\rho & 2\rho-1\\ \rho-1 & 1-\rho\end{pmatrix}$, trace $3-2\rho$;
$\mu^\top\Sigma_R^{-1}\mu = 9\cdot 1 = 9$; $\det\Sigma_\rho = 1-\rho^2$. So

$$
\operatorname{KL}(\pi_\rho|R) = \tfrac12\big[10 - 2\rho - \log(1-\rho^2)\big],
\qquad
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}: \ -2 + \frac{2\rho}{1-\rho^2} = 0
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \rho^2 + \rho - 1 = 0 .
$$

Hence $\rho^\star = \tfrac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2}\approx 0.618$, and the minimum is
$\tfrac12[10 - 1.236 + 0.481] \approx 4.62$. Two sanity checks. With $\rho = 0.5$ (the reference's
own correlation) the value is $4.644$, and with $\rho = 0.7$ it is $4.637$, both larger. And
$\rho^\star$ is the inverse golden ratio, so $1 - \rho^{\star2} = \rho^\star$, a relation we will
reuse. Cross-check against the general Gaussian entropic-OT formula: for unit variances and
cost $|x-y|^2$ with regularization $\varepsilon = 2$ the cross-covariance is
$\tfrac12\sqrt{4 + \varepsilon^2/4} - \varepsilon/4 = \tfrac12\sqrt5 - \tfrac12$. Same number.

**Why the stationarity condition is "no cross term".** Compute
$\log\pi_\rho - \log R$ as a quadratic in $(x,y)$: the $xy$ coefficient is
$\rho/(1-\rho^2) - 1$, which vanishes exactly when $\rho^2+\rho = 1$. At the optimum the ratio
$\pi^\star/R$ therefore factorizes as $a(x)\,b(y)$ with

$$
a(x)\propto\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1-\rho}{2}x^2 - 3x\big),
\qquad
b(y)\propto\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{\rho}{2}y^2 + 3(1+\rho)\,y\big),
$$

which is the Schrödinger-system structure from the [bridge page](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge):
the first-order condition of the KL minimization *is* the product form.

**Step 2: one IPF sweep.** Track the endpoint joint as (mean, covariance). Start at
$\pi^0 = R$: mean $(0,0)$, covariance $\Sigma_R$. Its terminal marginal is $\mathcal N(0,2)$, wrong
on both counts.

*First half-bridge (impose the terminal law).* Keep the reference's backward conditional
$X_0\mid X_1 = y\sim\mathcal N(y/2,\ 1/2)$ (Gaussian conditioning: slope $1/2$, residual
variance $1 - 1/2$) and re-start from $X_1\sim\mathcal N(3,1)$. Then $X_0 = X_1/2 + \eta$ with
$\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,1/2)$:

$$
\pi^1:\quad \mathbb{E} = (1.5,\ 3),\qquad \Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}0.75 & 0.5\\ 0.5 & 1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The terminal law is now correct and the initial law has drifted to $\mathcal N(1.5, 0.75)$. In path
space this iterate is the time reversal of Result 3: the reference has marginals
$\mathcal N(0,1+t)$, score $-y/(1+t)$, so $\pi^1$ reversed is
$\mathrm{d}Y_s = -Y_s/(2-s)\,\mathrm{d}s + \mathrm{d}\bar W_s$, $Y_0\sim\mathcal N(3,1)$. Its mean
solves $\dot m = -m/(2-s)$, $m(s) = 3(2-s)/2$, and $m(1) = 1.5$. Same answer.

*Second half-bridge (impose the initial law).* Keep $\pi^1$'s forward conditional
$X_1\mid X_0 = x\sim\mathcal N\big(3 + \tfrac{0.5}{0.75}(x-1.5),\ 1 - \tfrac{0.25}{0.75}\big) = \mathcal N\big(2 + \tfrac23x,\ \tfrac23\big)$
and re-start from $X_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$:

$$
\pi^2:\quad \mathbb{E} = (0,\ 2),\qquad \Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 2/3\\ 2/3 & 10/9\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The initial law is correct again; the terminal law is $\mathcal N(2, 1.11)$, much closer to
$\mathcal N(3,1)$ than the $\mathcal N(0,2)$ we started from. The correlation went
$0.707\to0.577\to0.632$, bracketing $0.618$.

Continuing, the forward slopes are $\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac35,\tfrac58,\tfrac8{13},\dots$, ratios of
consecutive Fibonacci numbers converging to $\rho^\star$ (Exercise 2), and the terminal-mean errors
are $3,\ 1,\ 0.375,\ 0.143,\ 0.055,\dots$, contracting by a factor approaching
$\rho^{\star2}\approx0.38$ per full sweep: geometric convergence, as the paper's Gaussian
appendix predicts.

**Step 3: the bridge drift.** By Result 1, $\pi^\star$ on paths is the optimal coupling glued to
Brownian bridges. Given endpoints $(x,y)$, a Brownian bridge on $[0,1]$ has drift
$(y - X_t)/(1-t)$, so the bridge's drift is

$$
u(t,x) = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_1\mid X_t = x] - x}{1-t}.
$$

Everything is jointly Gaussian: $X_t = (1-t)X_0 + tX_1 + \beta_t$ with $\beta_t\sim\mathcal N(0,t(1-t))$
independent of the endpoints, so $\mathbb{E}X_t = 3t$,
$v_t := \operatorname{Var}X_t = 1 + (2\rho-1)t(1-t)$, $c_t := \operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_1) = \rho + t(1-\rho)$,
and $\mathbb{E}[X_1\mid X_t = x] = 3 + (c_t/v_t)(x - 3t)$. Substituting and simplifying with
$\rho^2 = 1-\rho$ (so $2\rho - 1 = \rho^3$ and $1-\rho = \rho^2$) gives

$$
u(t,x) = \frac{3(1+\rho) - \rho\,x}{1 + \rho(1-t)}
\approx \frac{4.854 - 0.618\,x}{1.618 - 0.618\,t}.
$$

Linear in $x$ at every $t$, with coefficients that are smooth (rational) in $t$. Checks: at
$t=0$, $u = 3 - (1-\rho)x = 3 - 0.382x$, which is directly $\mathbb{E}[X_1\mid X_0 = x] - x$ from
the optimal coupling; at $(t,x) = (0.5, 0)$ the formula gives $3.708$, and the unsimplified
mixture formula gives $3.708$ as well. And it matches the Schrödinger-system form: the
bridge drift is $\nabla_x\log\varphi(t,x)$ with $\varphi(t,x) = \mathbb{E}[b(X_1)\mid X_t = x]$ under
the reference; for $b(y)\propto\exp(-\tfrac{\rho}{2}y^2 + 3(1+\rho)y)$ from Step 1 and a
$\mathcal N(x, 1-t)$ kernel, completing the square gives
$\nabla_x\log\varphi = \big(3(1+\rho) - \rho x\big)/\big(1 + \rho(1-t)\big)$. Same expression, from
the potential rather than from the mixture, so the two halves of Result 1 agree.
</Example>

## Reading order

1. [KL divergence and variational bounds](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds): the chain rule and strict convexity carry Results 1, 2, and 4.
2. [Girsanov's theorem](/concepts/girsanov-theorem): KL between path measures as a control cost.
3. [Time reversal of diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions): Anderson's formula, the engine of Result 3.
4. [Score matching](/concepts/score-matching): Tweedie's identity, which turns the reverse drift into a regression.
5. [Diffusion SDE framework](/concepts/diffusion-sde-framework): the model DSB generalizes, and the discretization conventions.
6. [Entropic OT and Sinkhorn](/concepts/entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn): the static problem and the iteration IPF is a copy of.
7. [Schrödinger bridge](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge): the static/dynamic dictionary and the potentials $(\varphi,\hat\varphi)$; read it last and then the paper's Section 3 will read as a summary.
