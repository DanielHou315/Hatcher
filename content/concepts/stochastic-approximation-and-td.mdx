## Why you need this

Every critic in deep RL is trained by a temporal-difference update: DQN, the twin critics of
SAC <Cite id="1801.01290" />, the value network in PPO, the Q-function in DPPO's critic. All of
them move a parameter a small step in the direction of a *noisy* estimate of a quantity whose
exact root they want, and all of the classical guarantees for doing that come from one 1951
paper by Robbins and Monro. The page proves the mean-square version of their theorem, explains
the ODE picture behind the step-size conditions $\sum\alpha_k=\infty$, $\sum\alpha_k^2<\infty$,
and shows that tabular TD(0) is a Robbins–Monro iteration whose root is $V^\pi$. Where deep RL
departs from the theory (constant steps, function approximation, off-policy data) is exactly
where it is known to be fragile; knowing the theorem tells you which departures matter.

## Setup

Let $h:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ be a function with a root $\theta^\star$, $h(\theta^\star)=0$, that we
cannot evaluate; we can only query $h(\theta)+\xi$ with zero-mean noise $\xi$. Work on a
probability space with a filtration $\mathcal F_0\subseteq\mathcal F_1\subseteq\cdots$, where
$\mathcal F_k$ is the information available after $k$ updates (the sigma-algebra generated by
$\theta_0,\xi_1,\dots,\xi_k$; see
[sigma-algebras as information](/concepts/measure-theoretic-probability)).

<Definition name="Robbins–Monro iteration">
$$
\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k+\alpha_k\big(h(\theta_k)+\xi_{k+1}\big),\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots
$$

with $\theta_k$ $\mathcal F_k$-measurable, deterministic step sizes $\alpha_k>0$, and
*martingale-difference* noise: $\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=0$. The step sizes satisfy the
Robbins–Monro conditions

$$
\sum_{k\ge0}\alpha_k=\infty,\qquad\sum_{k\ge0}\alpha_k^2<\infty .
$$

For example $\alpha_k=(k+1)^{-p}$ with $p\in(\tfrac12,1]$.
</Definition>

Two familiar instances: stochastic gradient descent, $h=-\nabla f$ with a minibatch gradient
as the noisy query; and the running average $\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k+\frac{1}{k+1}(X_{k+1}-\theta_k)$
of i.i.d. samples, which is the iteration with $h(\theta)=\mu-\theta$ and $\xi_{k+1}=X_{k+1}-\mu$.

## The idea

First, why the two step-size conditions. The iteration adds up two things: the deterministic
drift $\sum_k\alpha_kh(\theta_k)$, which needs $\sum\alpha_k=\infty$ to be able to move a finite
distance when $h$ is small, and the accumulated noise, which must stop moving eventually.

<Lemma name="Accumulated noise converges">
If $\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{k+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le\sigma^2$ and $\sum\alpha_k^2<\infty$, then
$M_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha_k\xi_{k+1}$ converges in $L^2$ (and almost surely).
</Lemma>

<Proof>
For $j>k$, the cross term satisfies
$\mathbb{E}\langle\alpha_k\xi_{k+1},\alpha_j\xi_{j+1}\rangle=\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha_k\alpha_j\langle\xi_{k+1},\mathbb{E}[\xi_{j+1}\mid\mathcal F_j]\rangle\big]=0$
by the tower property, since $\xi_{k+1}$ is $\mathcal F_j$-measurable. Hence for $m$ less than $n$,
$\mathbb{E}\|M_n-M_m\|^2=\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}\alpha_k^2\,\mathbb{E}\|\xi_{k+1}\|^2\le\sigma^2\sum_{k\ge m}\alpha_k^2\to0$.
So $(M_n)$ is Cauchy in $L^2$, which is complete. Almost-sure convergence is the martingale
convergence theorem for the $L^2$-bounded martingale $M_n$; we do not need it below. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Robbins–Monro, mean-square version" label="Thm 1">
Assume there are constants $c,L,K>0$ such that for all $\theta$ and $k$:

1. (strong monotonicity) $\langle\theta-\theta^\star,h(\theta)\rangle\le-c\|\theta-\theta^\star\|^2$;
2. (linear growth) $\|h(\theta)\|\le L\|\theta-\theta^\star\|$;
3. (noise) $\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{k+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le K(1+\|\theta_k-\theta^\star\|^2)$;
4. the Robbins–Monro step-size conditions, and $\mathbb{E}\|\theta_0\|^2<\infty$.

Then $\mathbb{E}\|\theta_k-\theta^\star\|^2\to0$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Write $e_k=\theta_k-\theta^\star$, so $e_{k+1}=e_k+\alpha_kh(\theta_k)+\alpha_k\xi_{k+1}$. Expand the
squared norm and take $\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\mid\mathcal F_k]$; the terms linear in $\xi_{k+1}$ vanish by
(3) because $e_k$ and $h(\theta_k)$ are $\mathcal F_k$-measurable:

$$
\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{k+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k\big]
=\|e_k\|^2+2\alpha_k\langle e_k,h(\theta_k)\rangle+\alpha_k^2\|h(\theta_k)\|^2+\alpha_k^2\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_{k+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k\big]
\le\big(1-2c\alpha_k+(L^2+K)\alpha_k^2\big)\|e_k\|^2+K\alpha_k^2 .
$$

Let $a_k=\mathbb{E}\|e_k\|^2$ (finite for every $k$ by induction from (4)). Since $\alpha_k\to0$, pick
$k_0$ with $(L^2+K)\alpha_k\le c$ for $k\ge k_0$; then $a_{k+1}\le(1-c\alpha_k)a_k+K\alpha_k^2$.
Unroll from $k_0$ to $n$ and use $1-x\le e^{-x}$:

$$
a_n\le a_{k_0}\exp\!\Big(-c\sum_{k=k_0}^{n-1}\alpha_k\Big)
+K\sum_{j=k_0}^{n-1}\alpha_j^2\exp\!\Big(-c\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}\alpha_i\Big).
$$

The first term tends to $0$ because $\sum\alpha_k=\infty$. For the second, fix $\varepsilon>0$ and
choose $m\ge k_0$ with $K\sum_{j\ge m}\alpha_j^2\le\varepsilon$. The part of the sum with $j\ge m$
is at most $\varepsilon$ (the exponentials are at most $1$). The part with $j$ less than $m$ is at
most $K\big(\sum_{j<m}\alpha_j^2\big)\exp\big(-c\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}\alpha_i\big)\to0$ as $n\to\infty$.
Hence $\limsup_na_n\le\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

The same hypotheses give $\theta_k\to\theta^\star$ almost surely. The proof replaces the expectation
recursion by the conditional one and applies the Robbins–Siegmund lemma, a supermartingale
convergence theorem; we skip it because it needs the supermartingale form of martingale
convergence, which is beyond the prerequisites.

<Intuition>
**The ODE method.** Set $t_k=\sum_{i<k}\alpha_i$ and interpolate $\theta_k$ linearly between the
times $t_k$. The iteration reads
$\theta_{k+1}-\theta_k=(t_{k+1}-t_k)\,h(\theta_k)+\alpha_k\xi_{k+1}$: an Euler step for the ODE
$\dot\theta=h(\theta)$ with a step that shrinks to zero, plus a noise whose partial sums converge
by the lemma. Over any finite window of ODE time the noise contributes vanishingly little, so
the interpolated iterates track the ODE flow, and $\sum\alpha_k=\infty$ says ODE time goes to
infinity. Condition (1) of the theorem is a Lyapunov condition for that ODE:
$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\theta-\theta^\star\|^2=2\langle\theta-\theta^\star,h(\theta)\rangle\le-2c\|\theta-\theta^\star\|^2$,
so the flow contracts to $\theta^\star$ exponentially. The general theorem (Kushner–Clark; Borkar
Ch. 2) states: if $h$ is Lipschitz, $\sup_k\|\theta_k\|$ is bounded almost surely, and the noise
is a martingale difference with bounded conditional variance, then $\theta_k$ converges almost
surely to an invariant set of the ODE, and to $\theta^\star$ when it is globally asymptotically
stable.
</Intuition>

**TD(0) as stochastic approximation.** Fix a policy $\pi$ on a finite MDP with $P_\pi$
irreducible, so it has a unique stationary distribution $d$ with $d(s)>0$ and $d^\top P_\pi=d^\top$.
Let $D=\operatorname{diag}(d)$ and $\|V\|_D^2=V^\top DV$. Rewards are bounded by $R_{\max}$. At step
$k$ we receive a transition $(s_k,r_k,s_k')$ with $s_k\sim d$, $s_k'\sim P_\pi(\cdot\mid s_k)$,
and $r_k$ with conditional mean $r_\pi(s_k)$, independently across $k$ (i.i.d. sampling from
the stationary chain). Writing $e_s$ for the unit vector of state $s$, tabular TD(0) is

$$
V_{k+1}=V_k+\alpha_k\,\delta_k\,e_{s_k},\qquad\delta_k=r_k+\gamma V_k(s_k')-V_k(s_k).
$$

<Proposition name="TD(0) is Robbins–Monro with root V^pi">
$\mathbb{E}[\delta_ke_{s_k}\mid\mathcal F_k]=D\,(T^\pi V_k-V_k)$, where $T^\pi$ is the Bellman expectation
operator. So TD(0) is the iteration of Thm 1 with $h(V)=D(T^\pi V-V)$, whose unique root is $V^\pi$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Given $\mathcal F_k$ (which fixes $V_k$), average over the sample:
$\sum_sd(s)e_s\big(r_\pi(s)+\gamma(P_\pi V_k)(s)-V_k(s)\big)=D(T^\pi V_k-V_k)$. The root is unique
because $D$ is invertible and $T^\pi$ has a unique fixed point. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Lemma name="P_pi is nonexpansive in the stationary norm">
$\|P_\pi V\|_D\le\|V\|_D$ for all $V$.
</Lemma>

<Proof>
By Jensen, $(P_\pi V)(s)^2=\big(\sum_{s'}P_\pi(s'\mid s)V(s')\big)^2\le\sum_{s'}P_\pi(s'\mid s)V(s')^2$.
Multiply by $d(s)$ and sum:
$\|P_\pi V\|_D^2\le\sum_{s'}\big(\sum_sd(s)P_\pi(s'\mid s)\big)V(s')^2=\sum_{s'}d(s')V(s')^2=\|V\|_D^2$
by stationarity. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Convergence of tabular TD(0)" label="Thm 2">
Under i.i.d. sampling from the stationary chain and Robbins–Monro step sizes,
$\mathbb{E}\|V_k-V^\pi\|^2\to0$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Check the hypotheses of Thm 1 with $e=V-V^\pi$. Using $T^\pi V-T^\pi V^\pi=\gamma P_\pi e$,

$$
\langle e,h(V)\rangle=e^\top D(\gamma P_\pi e-e)=\gamma\langle e,P_\pi e\rangle_D-\|e\|_D^2
\le\gamma\|e\|_D\|P_\pi e\|_D-\|e\|_D^2\le-(1-\gamma)\|e\|_D^2\le-(1-\gamma)\,d_{\min}\|e\|^2 ,
$$

by Cauchy–Schwarz in the $D$-inner product and the lemma; this is (1) with
$c=(1-\gamma)d_{\min}$. (2): $h(V)=D(\gamma P_\pi-I)e$ is linear in $e$, so $L$ is the operator
norm of $D(\gamma P_\pi-I)$. (3): the noise $\xi=\delta e_s-h(V)$ satisfies
$\|\xi\|^2\le2\delta^2+2\|h(V)\|^2$ and $|\delta|\le R_{\max}+(1+\gamma)(\|V^\pi\|_\infty+\|e\|_\infty)$,
so $\mathbb{E}[\|\xi\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le K(1+\|e\|^2)$ for some $K$. Thm 1 applies. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Warning>
Three things the theorem does not cover, each a known failure mode.

1. **Markov sampling.** Along an actual trajectory the samples are not independent and the
   noise is not a martingale difference. The conclusion still holds (Tsitsiklis and Van Roy
   1997) but needs the ODE method with Markov noise and a mixing-time argument.
2. **Function approximation.** With $V=\Phi w$ linear, the same $D$-norm argument shows
   on-policy TD(0) converges to the fixed point of $\Pi_D T^\pi$, the Bellman operator followed by
   $D$-weighted projection. The lemma used stationarity of $d$; with *off-policy* data the
   weighting is wrong, $\Pi_DT^\pi$ need not be a contraction, and TD can diverge (Baird's
   counterexample). Off-policy plus bootstrapping plus function approximation is the "deadly
   triad"; DQN's replay buffer and target network are engineering mitigations, not a proof.
3. **Q-learning.** The optimality operator $T$ is a sup-norm contraction but not monotone in an
   inner product, so Thm 1 does not apply as stated. Its convergence (Tsitsiklis 1994;
   Jaakkola, Jordan and Singh 1994) uses a sup-norm version of stochastic approximation with
   asynchronous updates.
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="TD(0) on a two-state chain">
States $\{A,B\}$, a fixed policy, $\gamma=\tfrac12$. From $A$ the chain always moves to $B$ and
earns $r=1$. From $B$ it earns $r=0$ and moves to $A$ or stays at $B$ with probability $\tfrac12$
each.

**Exact values.** $V(A)=1+\tfrac12V(B)$ and $V(B)=0+\tfrac12\big(\tfrac12V(A)+\tfrac12V(B)\big)$. The
second gives $\tfrac34V(B)=\tfrac14V(A)$, so $V(B)=V(A)/3$; then $V(A)=1+V(A)/6$, so
$V^\pi(A)=\tfrac65=1.2$ and $V^\pi(B)=0.4$.

**Stationary distribution.** $d(A)=\tfrac12d(B)$ and $d(A)+d(B)=1$ give $d=(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$.
Check the lemma on $V=(1,0)$: $P_\pi V=(0,\tfrac12)$, $\|P_\pi V\|_D^2=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac16$
and $\|V\|_D^2=\tfrac13$. Nonexpansive, as promised.

**Six TD(0) updates** from $V_0=(0,0)$ with constant $\alpha=\tfrac12$ (to keep the arithmetic
short; see the remark after), along the sampled path $A\to B\to B\to A\to B\to A\to B$:

1. $s=A$, $r=1$, $s'=B$: $\delta=1+\tfrac12\cdot0-0=1$. $V(A)\leftarrow0+\tfrac12\cdot1=0.5$. $V=(0.5,\,0)$.
2. $s=B$, $r=0$, $s'=B$: $\delta=0+\tfrac12\cdot0-0=0$. No change. $V=(0.5,\,0)$.
3. $s=B$, $r=0$, $s'=A$: $\delta=\tfrac12\cdot0.5-0=0.25$. $V(B)\leftarrow0+0.125=0.125$. $V=(0.5,\,0.125)$.
4. $s=A$, $r=1$, $s'=B$: $\delta=1+\tfrac12\cdot0.125-0.5=0.5625$. $V(A)\leftarrow0.5+0.28125=0.78125$.
5. $s=B$, $r=0$, $s'=A$: $\delta=\tfrac12\cdot0.78125-0.125=0.265625$. $V(B)\leftarrow0.125+0.1328=0.2578$.
6. $s=A$, $r=1$, $s'=B$: $\delta=1+\tfrac12\cdot0.2578-0.78125=0.3477$. $V(A)\leftarrow0.78125+0.1738=0.9551$.

Final $V_6=(0.955,\,0.258)$ against $V^\pi=(1.2,\,0.4)$. The sup-norm error went
$1.2\to0.7\to0.7\to0.7\to0.419\to0.419\to0.245$: it never increased along this path, and the
ratio $V(B)/V(A)=0.27$ is approaching the exact $\tfrac13$.

**Sanity check of the fixed point.** At $V=V^\pi$ the expected TD error must vanish at every state:
$\mathbb{E}[\delta\mid s=A]=1+\tfrac12\cdot0.4-1.2=0$ and
$\mathbb{E}[\delta\mid s=B]=\tfrac12\big(\tfrac12\cdot1.2+\tfrac12\cdot0.4\big)-0.4=0.4-0.4=0$. So
$h(V^\pi)=0$, as the proposition says.

**On the constant step.** With $\alpha$ fixed, the iterates do not converge; they keep
fluctuating around $V^\pi$ with a spread proportional to $\sqrt\alpha$ (the noise term
$\alpha\xi$ never dies). The Robbins–Monro choice, for instance $\alpha_k=1/(n_k(s)+1)$ with
$n_k(s)$ the visit count, removes the fluctuation at the cost of reacting slowly to change.
Deep RL keeps a constant step (Adam) and accepts the fluctuation, using target networks and
averaging to tame it.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **DQN and SAC critics.** The target $r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\theta^-}(s',a')$ (DQN) or
  $r+\gamma\big(Q_{\theta^-}(s',a')-\alpha\log\pi(a'\mid s')\big)$ (the soft target of SAC <Cite id="1801.01290" />)
  is a one-sample Bellman backup; the gradient step on the squared residual is the
  Robbins–Monro increment with $h=$ (projected) Bellman residual. The target network freezes
  $\theta^-$ so that the update is a genuine stochastic approximation to a fixed operator, and its
  Polyak update $\theta^-\leftarrow(1-\tau)\theta^-+\tau\theta$ is itself a Robbins–Monro
  averaging with constant step.
- **Actor–critic.** The critic runs TD; the actor runs a policy gradient on a slower step size.
  Convergence proofs (Konda and Tsitsiklis 2000) are two-time-scale stochastic approximation:
  the fast iterate sees the slow one as frozen, the slow one sees the fast one as converged.
- **GAE and PPO's value loss.** The value network is fitted by regression on
  $\lambda$-returns, which is TD($\lambda$) in batch form; the same projected fixed point
  analysis applies.
- **Tabular guarantees behind theory papers.** Finite-sample results for TD and Q-learning
  start from Thm 1 or its sup-norm cousin and add concentration bounds on the noise; see
  [concentration inequalities](/concepts/concentration-inequalities).

## Exercises

<Exercise hint="The p-series sum of 1/k^q converges exactly when q exceeds 1.">
Show that $\alpha_k=(k+1)^{-p}$ satisfies both Robbins–Monro conditions if and only if
$\tfrac12$ is less than $p\le1$. What goes wrong with $p=\tfrac12$? With $p=1.5$?
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Strong convexity gives the inner-product inequality directly; Lipschitz gradients give linear growth.">
Let $f$ be $c$-strongly convex with $L$-Lipschitz gradient and minimizer $\theta^\star$. Show that
$h=-\nabla f$ satisfies hypotheses (1) and (2) of Thm 1, so SGD with Robbins–Monro steps and
bounded-variance minibatch noise converges in mean square.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="h(theta) = mu − theta is linear; c = L = 1, and the noise variance is constant.">
Verify that the running average $\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k+\frac{1}{k+1}(X_{k+1}-\theta_k)$ of i.i.d.
samples with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ satisfies Thm 1, and show directly that
$\theta_k$ is the sample mean, so Thm 1 reduces to the $L^2$ law of large numbers.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Continue the table with the same rule; then compare the sup-norm error with 0.245.">
Continue the worked example for three more transitions along $B\to B\to A\to B$ with
$\alpha=\tfrac12$. Then redo the six original updates with $\alpha_k=1/(n_k(s)+1)$ and compare.
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Borkar Ch. 2 gives the ODE method in full with the martingale-noise assumptions above, and
Ch. 6 the two-time-scale version used for actor–critic. Tsitsiklis and Van Roy (1997) is the
canonical proof of TD($\lambda$) with linear function approximation and Markov sampling, and
contains the divergence example for off-policy data. Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis Ch. 4 prove the
sup-norm stochastic approximation theorem that covers Q-learning. Kushner and Yin,
*Stochastic Approximation and Recursive Algorithms and Applications*, is the reference for
constant-step-size behavior (weak convergence to a stationary distribution around the root).
