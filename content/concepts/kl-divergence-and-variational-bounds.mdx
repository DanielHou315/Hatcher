## Why you need this

The DDPM training objective of Ho et al. 2020 is a sum of KL divergences between Gaussians,
one per noise level; that sum is an evidence lower bound obtained from the chain rule of KL
applied along a Markov chain. The continuous-time version (Song, Durkan, Murray, Ermon 2021)
bounds the negative log-likelihood of a diffusion model by a weighted score-matching loss,
using KL between path measures. Schrödinger bridges (De Bortoli et al. 2021) are defined as
KL projections onto a constraint set. In RL, SAC's policy improvement step, the KL penalty in
PPO-style fine-tuning, and every "KL-regularized" objective are instances of one variational
identity, Donsker–Varadhan. This page proves those identities once so the rest of the site can
cite them.

## Setup

Let $P$ and $Q$ be probability measures on the same space $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$. We write
$P \ll Q$ ($P$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $Q$) when $Q(A)=0$ implies $P(A)=0$;
then the Radon–Nikodym derivative $\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}Q$ exists
(see [measure-theoretic probability](/concepts/measure-theoretic-probability)). When both
have densities $p, q$ with respect to a common reference measure (Lebesgue on $\mathbb{R}^d$,
counting measure on a finite set), $\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}Q = p/q$ on the set where $q>0$.
All logarithms are natural.

<Definition name="Kullback–Leibler divergence (relative entropy)">
$$
\operatorname{KL}(P\,\|\,Q) =
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{E}_P\!\left[\log \dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}\right]
= \displaystyle\int p \log\frac{p}{q}, & P \ll Q,\\[1.2em]
+\infty, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
The convention $0\log 0 = 0$ applies where $p = 0$. The integral is well defined in
$(-\infty,+\infty]$: the negative part of $p\log(p/q)$ is bounded by $q/e$, which is integrable.
</Definition>

## The idea

<Proposition name="Nonnegativity (Gibbs' inequality)" label="Prop 1">
$\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q)\ge 0$, with equality if and only if $P = Q$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Assume $P\ll Q$ (otherwise KL is $+\infty$). Jensen's inequality for the convex function
$-\log$ gives
$$
\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) = \mathbb{E}_P\!\left[-\log\frac{q}{p}\right]
\;\ge\; -\log \mathbb{E}_P\!\left[\frac{q}{p}\right]
= -\log \int_{\{p>0\}} q \;\ge\; -\log 1 = 0 .
$$
Strict convexity of $-\log$ makes the first inequality an equality only if $q/p$ is
$P$-a.s. constant, and the second only if $Q(\{p>0\}) = 1$; together these force
$q = p$ a.e. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Warning>
KL is not symmetric and does not satisfy a triangle inequality. $\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q)$
penalises regions where $P$ has mass and $Q$ does not (the integrand blows up when $q\to 0$
with $p>0$); it ignores regions where $Q$ has mass and $P$ does not. Minimising
$\operatorname{KL}(Q_\theta\|P)$ over $\theta$ therefore prefers $Q_\theta$ that stay inside the
support of $P$ (mode seeking); minimising $\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q_\theta)$, which is what
maximum likelihood does, prefers $Q_\theta$ that cover all of $P$ (mass covering).
</Warning>

<Theorem name="Chain rule" label="Thm 1">
Let $P_{XY}, Q_{XY}$ be joint laws on $\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y$ with marginals $P_X,Q_X$
and regular conditionals $P_{Y|X=x}, Q_{Y|X=x}$. Then
$$
\operatorname{KL}(P_{XY}\|Q_{XY})
= \operatorname{KL}(P_X\|Q_X) + \mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_X}\big[\operatorname{KL}(P_{Y|X=x}\|Q_{Y|X=x})\big].
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
We give the density case; the general case replaces densities by Radon–Nikodym derivatives
and uses that the conditional density of a joint density is a version of the regular
conditional. If $P_{XY}\not\ll Q_{XY}$ then some term on the right is infinite too (check:
if $P_X\ll Q_X$ and $P_{Y|X=x}\ll Q_{Y|X=x}$ for $P_X$-a.e. $x$ then $P_{XY}\ll Q_{XY}$), so
assume absolute continuity. Write $p(x,y) = p(x)\,p(y|x)$ and $q(x,y) = q(x)\,q(y|x)$. Then
$$
\log\frac{p(x,y)}{q(x,y)} = \log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} + \log\frac{p(y|x)}{q(y|x)} .
$$
Take $\mathbb{E}_{P_{XY}}$ of both sides. The first term depends on $x$ only, giving
$\operatorname{KL}(P_X\|Q_X)$. For the second use the tower property
(see [conditional expectation](/concepts/conditional-expectation)):
$\mathbb{E}_{P_{XY}}[\,\cdot\,] = \mathbb{E}_{P_X}\big[\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|X}}[\,\cdot\,]\big]$,
and the inner expectation is exactly $\operatorname{KL}(P_{Y|X=x}\|Q_{Y|X=x})$. Both terms are
nonnegative by Prop 1, so no $\infty-\infty$ can occur. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

Iterating along a Markov chain $x_0\to x_1\to\dots\to x_T$ gives
$\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) = \operatorname{KL}(P_{x_T}\|Q_{x_T}) + \sum_t \mathbb{E}\,\operatorname{KL}(P_{x_{t-1}|x_t}\|Q_{x_{t-1}|x_t})$,
which is the DDPM decomposition (Exercise 4).

<Corollary name="Data processing inequality" label="Cor 2">
Let $K$ be a Markov kernel from $\mathcal X$ to $\mathcal Y$ and $P_Y = P_XK$, $Q_Y = Q_XK$
(push $P_X$ and $Q_X$ through the same channel). Then
$\operatorname{KL}(P_Y\|Q_Y)\le\operatorname{KL}(P_X\|Q_X)$.
</Corollary>

<Proof>
Apply Thm 1 to the joint laws $P_{XY} = P_X\otimes K$ and $Q_{XY} = Q_X\otimes K$ in both
orders. Conditioning on $X$ first: the conditionals $P_{Y|X}$ and $Q_{Y|X}$ are both $K$,
so their KL is zero and $\operatorname{KL}(P_{XY}\|Q_{XY}) = \operatorname{KL}(P_X\|Q_X)$.
Conditioning on $Y$ first:
$\operatorname{KL}(P_{XY}\|Q_{XY}) = \operatorname{KL}(P_Y\|Q_Y) + \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{KL}(P_{X|Y}\|Q_{X|Y})]\ge\operatorname{KL}(P_Y\|Q_Y)$.
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

Deterministic maps are kernels, so $\operatorname{KL}(f_\#P\|f_\#Q)\le\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q)$
for any measurable $f$: you cannot make two distributions more distinguishable by processing
them. Marginalising is a special case.

<Theorem name="Donsker–Varadhan variational formula" label="Thm 3">
$$
\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) = \sup_{f}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_P[f] - \log\mathbb{E}_Q\big[e^{f}\big]\Big\},
$$
the supremum over measurable $f$ with $\mathbb{E}_Q[e^f]<\infty$ (bounded $f$ suffice). When
$\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q)<\infty$ the supremum is attained at $f^\star = \log\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}Q$,
unique up to an additive constant.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Fix $f$ and set $Z = \mathbb{E}_Q[e^f]$. Define the tilted measure $Q_f$ by
$\mathrm{d}Q_f/\mathrm{d}Q = e^{f}/Z$, a probability measure equivalent to $Q$. If $P\ll Q$ then
$P\ll Q_f$ and
$$
\log\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q_f} = \log\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q} - f + \log Z .
$$
Taking $\mathbb{E}_P$:
$\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q_f) = \operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) - \mathbb{E}_P[f] + \log Z$.
The left side is $\ge 0$ by Prop 1, which is the inequality $\ge$ in the theorem, with equality
iff $P = Q_f$, i.e. $f = \log\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}Q + \text{const}$. If that $f^\star$ is not
bounded, apply the identity to $f_n = \max(\min(f^\star,n),-n)$ and let $n\to\infty$ by monotone
convergence to get the supremum over bounded functions. If $P\not\ll Q$, pick $A$ with
$Q(A) = 0$, $P(A)>0$ and $f = n\mathbf 1_A$: then $\mathbb{E}_P f - \log\mathbb{E}_Q e^f = nP(A)\to\infty$,
matching $\operatorname{KL} = +\infty$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Corollary name="Gibbs variational principle" label="Cor 4">
For a reference measure $\pi_0$ and a cost $c$ with $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[e^{-c}]<\infty$,
$$
\min_{\pi}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_\pi[c] + \operatorname{KL}(\pi\|\pi_0)\Big\}
= -\log\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}\big[e^{-c}\big],
\qquad
\pi^\star(\mathrm{d}x) = \frac{e^{-c(x)}\,\pi_0(\mathrm{d}x)}{\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[e^{-c}]} .
$$
</Corollary>

<Proof>
Thm 3 with $P = \pi$, $Q = \pi_0$, $f = -c$ reads
$\operatorname{KL}(\pi\|\pi_0)\ge -\mathbb{E}_\pi[c] - \log\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[e^{-c}]$, i.e.
$\mathbb{E}_\pi[c] + \operatorname{KL}(\pi\|\pi_0)\ge -\log\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[e^{-c}]$, with
equality iff $\pi = Q_f = \pi^\star$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

This is the whole of "soft" RL in one line: with $c = -Q(s,\cdot)/\alpha$ and
$\pi_0$ uniform, the optimal policy is Boltzmann in $Q$
(see [KL-regularized RL](/concepts/kl-regularized-rl)).

<Theorem name="Evidence lower bound" label="Thm 5">
Let $p(x,z) = p(z)\,p(x|z)$ be a latent-variable model with marginal $p(x)=\int p(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}z$
and let $q(z|x)$ be any density in $z$ with $q(\cdot|x)\ll p(\cdot|x)$. Then
$$
\log p(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}\!\left[\log\frac{p(x,z)}{q(z|x)}\right]}_{\mathrm{ELBO}(q;x)}
+ \operatorname{KL}\big(q(\cdot|x)\,\|\,p(\cdot|x)\big),
$$
so $\log p(x)\ge\mathrm{ELBO}(q;x)$ with equality iff $q(z|x) = p(z|x)$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
$\log p(x)$ does not depend on $z$, so $\log p(x) = \mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}[\log p(x)]$. Write
$p(x) = p(x,z)/p(z|x)$ and insert $q(z|x)/q(z|x)$:
$$
\log p(x)
= \mathbb{E}_{q}\!\left[\log\frac{p(x,z)}{q(z|x)}\right]
+ \mathbb{E}_{q}\!\left[\log\frac{q(z|x)}{p(z|x)}\right].
$$
The second term is $\operatorname{KL}(q\|p(\cdot|x))\ge 0$ by Prop 1. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Intuition>
The ELBO is Thm 3 in disguise: with $Q = p(z)$, $f = \log p(x|z)$,
Donsker–Varadhan says $\log p(x) = \log\mathbb{E}_{p(z)}[e^{f}]\ge \mathbb{E}_q[f] - \operatorname{KL}(q\|p(z))$,
and the right side is the ELBO written as "reconstruction minus KL to the prior". Every
variational bound on this site is one of these two rearrangements.
</Intuition>

**KL between path measures.** If $P^1, P^2$ are the laws on $C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^d)$ of
$\mathrm{d}X_t = b_i(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ with the same initial law and
constant $\sigma>0$, then under Novikov-type conditions
$$
\operatorname{KL}(P^1\|P^2) = \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\,\mathbb{E}_{P^1}\!\int_0^T \|b_1(X_t,t)-b_2(X_t,t)\|^2\,\mathrm{d}t .
$$
This is Girsanov's theorem plus the definition of KL; the derivation is on the
[Girsanov](/concepts/girsanov-theorem) page. It is the continuous-time chain rule: the
per-step Gaussian KLs of DDPM become an integral of squared drift differences, which is why
the diffusion NLL bound is a weighted score-matching loss
(see [the diffusion SDE framework](/concepts/diffusion-sde-framework)).

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="A three-point distribution: Jensen and Donsker–Varadhan checked numerically">
Let $P = (0.5, 0.3, 0.2)$ and $Q = (\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ on three points.

*KL.* $\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) = \sum_i p_i\log(3p_i) = \log 3 - H(P)$ with
$H(P) = -\sum p_i\log p_i = 0.3466 + 0.3612 + 0.3219 = 1.0297$, so
$\operatorname{KL}(P\|Q) = 1.0986 - 1.0297 = 0.0689$.

*Asymmetry.* $\operatorname{KL}(Q\|P) = \tfrac13\big[\log\tfrac{1/3}{0.5}+\log\tfrac{1/3}{0.3}+\log\tfrac{1/3}{0.2}\big]
= \tfrac13[-0.4055 + 0.1054 + 0.5108] = 0.0702 \ne 0.0689$.

*Jensen step of Prop 1.* $\mathbb{E}_P[q/p] = 0.5\cdot\tfrac{1/3}{0.5} + 0.3\cdot\tfrac{1/3}{0.3} + 0.2\cdot\tfrac{1/3}{0.2} = \tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac13 = 1$,
so $-\log\mathbb{E}_P[q/p] = 0$, while $\mathbb{E}_P[-\log(q/p)] = 0.0689\ge 0$. The Jensen gap
is the divergence itself.

*Donsker–Varadhan.* Take the test function $f = (1,0,0)$: $\mathbb{E}_P f = 0.5$,
$\mathbb{E}_Q e^f = (e+1+1)/3 = 1.5728$, $\log = 0.4528$, bound $= 0.5-0.4528 = 0.0472\le 0.0689$.
Now the optimal $f^\star = \log(p/q) = (0.4055,-0.1054,-0.5108)$:
$\mathbb{E}_P f^\star = 0.2027-0.0316-0.1022 = 0.0689$ and
$\mathbb{E}_Q e^{f^\star} = \tfrac13(1.5+0.9+0.6) = 1$, so the bound is $0.0689$, tight.
</Example>

<Example title="ELBO for a one-dimensional Gaussian latent model">
Model: $z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, $x\mid z\sim\mathcal N(z,1)$. Then $x\sim\mathcal N(0,2)$ and
the exact posterior is $z\mid x\sim\mathcal N(x/2,\,1/2)$
(Gaussian conditioning; see [Gaussian computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations)).
Variational family: $q(z|x) = \mathcal N(m,s^2)$.

Compute the three terms of the ELBO using $\mathbb{E}_q[(x-z)^2] = (x-m)^2+s^2$,
$\mathbb{E}_q[z^2] = m^2+s^2$, and the Gaussian entropy $-\mathbb{E}_q[\log q] = \tfrac12\log(2\pi e s^2)$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ELBO}(m,s) &= \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x|z)] + \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(z)] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(z|x)] \\
&= -\tfrac12\log 2\pi - \tfrac12\big[(x-m)^2+s^2\big]
   -\tfrac12\log 2\pi - \tfrac12\big[m^2+s^2\big]
   +\tfrac12\log 2\pi + \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\log s^2 \\
&= -\tfrac12\log 2\pi - \tfrac12(x-m)^2 - \tfrac12 m^2 - s^2 + \tfrac12\log s^2 + \tfrac12 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Maximise: $\partial_m = (x-m) - m = 0\Rightarrow m^\star = x/2$;
$\partial_s = -2s + 1/s = 0\Rightarrow s^{\star2} = 1/2$. These are exactly the posterior
parameters, as Thm 5 predicts (the KL term vanishes iff $q$ is the posterior).

Numbers at $x = 2$: $m^\star = 1$, $s^{\star 2} = \tfrac12$, so
$\mathrm{ELBO} = -\tfrac12\log 2\pi - \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\log 2 + \tfrac12
= -\tfrac12\log 4\pi - 1 = -2.2655$. Directly, $\log p(2) = -\tfrac12\log(4\pi) - \tfrac{2^2}{4} = -2.2655$. Equal.

Now a bad $q$: the prior itself, $m = 0$, $s^2 = 1$:
$\mathrm{ELBO} = -\tfrac12\log 2\pi - 2 - 0 - 1 + 0 + \tfrac12 = -3.4189$. The gap is
$-2.2655 + 3.4189 = 1.1534$. Thm 5 says this must equal
$\operatorname{KL}(\mathcal N(0,1)\,\|\,\mathcal N(1,\tfrac12))
= \log\tfrac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1} + \tfrac{\sigma_1^2+(\mu_1-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2} - \tfrac12
= -0.3466 + \tfrac{1+1}{1} - 0.5 = 1.1534$. It does.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **DDPM** <Cite id="2006.11239" />: the loss $L = L_T + \sum_{t>1}L_{t-1} + L_0$ with
  $L_{t-1} = \operatorname{KL}\big(q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)\,\|\,p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)\big)$ is Thm 5
  with $z = x_{1:T}$ followed by Thm 1 along the reverse Markov chain. Each $L_{t-1}$ is a
  KL between Gaussians with the same covariance, hence a squared error in the means, hence
  the $\epsilon$-prediction loss.
- **Likelihood of score-based models** <Cite id="2101.09258" />: Theorem 1 there bounds
  $-\mathbb{E}\log p_\theta(x)$ by the score-matching loss with weighting $g(t)^2$ plus
  $\operatorname{KL}(p_T\|\pi)$; the proof is the path-measure KL above, followed by Cor 2
  (marginalising the path to its endpoint can only decrease KL).
- **VAEs** <Cite id="1312.6114" />: Thm 5 with the reparametrised Gaussian $q_\phi(z|x)$;
  the KL-to-prior term is the closed-form Gaussian KL from the second example.
- **Schrödinger bridges** ([page](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge)): the dynamic problem is
  $\min\operatorname{KL}(P\|P^{\mathrm{ref}})$ over path measures with fixed marginals; the
  chain rule (Thm 1) splits it into a static entropic-OT problem plus a bridge term, and the
  IPF iterations of De Bortoli et al. 2021 are alternating KL projections.
- **KL-regularized RL** ([page](/concepts/kl-regularized-rl)): Cor 4 is the soft policy
  improvement step of SAC, the closed-form optimal policy of RLHF-style objectives
  $\max\mathbb{E}_\pi[r] - \beta\operatorname{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$, and the reason the
  soft value is a log-partition function. PPO's clipped ratio is a surrogate for a trust
  region measured in KL.

</Exercise>

$L_T + \sum_{t>1}\operatorname{KL}(q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)\|p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)) - \mathbb{E}\log p_\theta(x_0|x_1)$.
Which step uses the Markov property of $q$?
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Cover and Thomas Ch. 2 proves Prop 1, Thm 1 and Cor 2 for discrete alphabets in a few pages.
Polyanskiy and Wu Ch. 2 and 4 do the general measure-theoretic case, including the
Donsker–Varadhan formula and its role in large deviations. Kingma and Welling's VAE paper is
the cleanest short account of the ELBO as an optimisation target. For KL between diffusion
path measures read the [Girsanov](/concepts/girsanov-theorem) page and then Appendix A of
Song, Durkan, Murray and Ermon.
