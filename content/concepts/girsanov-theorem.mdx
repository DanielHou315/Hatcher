## Why you need this

Two SDEs with the same noise but different drifts produce path distributions that are mutually
absolutely continuous, and Girsanov's theorem writes down the density of one with respect to the
other. Take a logarithm and an expectation and you get the KL divergence between the two path
measures as $\tfrac12\int\mathbb{E}\|\text{drift difference}\|^2/g^2\,\mathrm{d}t$. That single
formula is the variational bound of <Cite id="2101.09258" /> (their Theorem 1: the score-matching
loss upper-bounds negative log-likelihood), the objective of the dynamic
[Schrödinger bridge](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge) and of <Cite id="2106.01357" />, and the
KL penalty in RL fine-tuning of diffusion policies, where the fine-tuned and base samplers are two
reverse SDEs differing only in drift.

## Setup

Work on $[0,T]$ with a $d$-dimensional Brownian motion $W$ under a probability measure
$\mathbb{P}$, with its filtration $(\mathcal{F}_t)$. Let $\theta_t\in\mathbb{R}^d$ be an adapted
process with $\int_0^T\|\theta_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t$ finite almost surely.

Recall from the [KL page](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds): if $\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}$
with density $Z = \mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}$, then
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[Y] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[ZY]$ and
$\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{Q}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\log Z]$. Here $\mathbb{P}$ and
$\mathbb{Q}$ will be measures on the space of continuous paths, and $Z$ will be a functional of
the whole path.

<Definition name="Stochastic exponential">
$$
Z_t = \exp\!\Big(\int_0^t\theta_s^{\top}\mathrm{d}W_s - \tfrac12\int_0^t\|\theta_s\|^2\mathrm{d}s\Big),
\qquad 0\le t\le T .
$$
</Definition>

<Lemma name="Z is a positive local martingale" label="Lemma 1">
$\mathrm{d}Z_t = Z_t\,\theta_t^{\top}\mathrm{d}W_t$, so $Z$ is a nonnegative local martingale, hence
a supermartingale with $\mathbb{E}Z_T\le 1$. It is a true martingale on $[0,T]$ if and only if
$\mathbb{E}Z_T = 1$.
</Lemma>

<Proof>
Let $Y_t = \int_0^t\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}W - \tfrac12\int_0^t\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}s$, so
$\mathrm{d}Y = \theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}W - \tfrac12\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t$ and
$\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}Y = \|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t$. [Itô's lemma](/concepts/ito-lemma) for
$f(y) = e^y$ gives $\mathrm{d}Z = Z\,\mathrm{d}Y + \tfrac12 Z\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}Y = Z\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}W - \tfrac12 Z\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t + \tfrac12 Z\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t$.
The $\mathrm{d}t$ terms cancel; that is what the $-\tfrac12\int\|\theta\|^2$ in the definition is for.
A stochastic integral is a local martingale; Fatou's lemma turns a nonnegative local martingale
into a supermartingale; and a supermartingale with constant expectation is a martingale. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Novikov's condition" label="Thm 2">
If $\mathbb{E}\exp\!\big(\tfrac12\int_0^T\|\theta_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t\big)$ is finite, then $Z$ is a
martingale on $[0,T]$. In particular this holds whenever $\theta$ is bounded.
</Theorem>

We use Novikov as a black box; Karatzas and Shreve 3.5.D has the proof. In every example on this
site $\theta$ is either bounded or Gaussian with bounded variance, and Novikov is checked in one line.

## The idea

<Theorem name="Girsanov" label="Thm 3">
Suppose $Z$ is a martingale on $[0,T]$ and define $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathcal{F}_T$ by
$\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q} = Z_T\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}$. Then

$$
\tilde W_t = W_t - \int_0^t\theta_s\,\mathrm{d}s , \qquad 0\le t\le T,
$$

is a $d$-dimensional Brownian motion under $\mathbb{Q}$.
</Theorem>

<Intuition>
Under $\mathbb{P}$, $W$ has no drift. Reweighting paths by $Z_T$ favours paths that move along
$\theta$: the weight $e^{\int\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}W}$ is large exactly when the increments of
$W$ align with $\theta$. Girsanov says the reweighted process looks precisely like a Brownian
motion plus the drift $\theta$, no more and no less, and the $-\tfrac12\int\|\theta\|^2$ term is
the normalisation that keeps total mass one.
</Intuition>

The proof uses two tools. The first is a Bayes rule for conditional expectations under a change
of measure; the second is Lévy's characterisation of Brownian motion, which we quote.

<Lemma name="Bayes rule under a change of measure" label="Lemma 4">
For $s\le t\le T$ and $Y$ bounded and $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable,
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_tY\mid\mathcal{F}_s]/Z_s$.
</Lemma>

<Proof>
Let $A\in\mathcal{F}_s$. Then $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[Y\mathbf{1}_A] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_TY\mathbf{1}_A] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_tY\mathbf{1}_A]$ by the tower property and the martingale property of $Z$,
since $Y\mathbf{1}_A$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. Conditioning on $\mathcal{F}_s$,
this equals $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\big[\mathbf{1}_A\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_tY\mid\mathcal{F}_s]\big] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\big[Z_s\mathbf{1}_A\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_tY\mid\mathcal{F}_s]/Z_s\big] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\mathbf{1}_A\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_tY\mid\mathcal{F}_s]/Z_s\big]$,
using $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[X] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_sX]$ for $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable $X$.
That is the defining property of the conditional expectation. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**Lévy's characterisation** (Karatzas and Shreve 3.3.16): a continuous process $M$ with $M_0 = 0$
that is a local martingale and has quadratic covariation $[M^{i},M^{j}]_t = \delta_{ij}\,t$ is a
Brownian motion. This is the converse of "Brownian motion has $[W]_t = t$"; we quote it.

<Proof>
(Of Thm 3.) Take $d = 1$ to lighten notation; the vector case is identical componentwise.

**Step 1: quadratic variation.** $\tilde W = W - \int\theta\,\mathrm{d}s$ differs from $W$ by a
$C^1$ process, which has zero quadratic variation, so $[\tilde W]_t = [W]_t = t$ almost surely
under $\mathbb{P}$. Since $\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}$, an almost-sure statement under $\mathbb{P}$ is
almost sure under $\mathbb{Q}$. (Quadratic variation is a limit of sums along the path; it does
not depend on which measure you use to talk about the path.)

**Step 2: $\tilde W$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-local martingale.** By Lemma 4 it suffices to show that
$Z\tilde W$ is a $\mathbb{P}$-local martingale, because then
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\tilde W_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[Z_t\tilde W_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s]/Z_s = Z_s\tilde W_s/Z_s = \tilde W_s$
(with localisation handled by stopping times). Itô's product rule with $\mathrm{d}Z = Z\theta\,\mathrm{d}W$
and $\mathrm{d}\tilde W = \mathrm{d}W - \theta\,\mathrm{d}t$:

$$
\mathrm{d}(Z\tilde W) = Z\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W + \tilde W\,\mathrm{d}Z + \mathrm{d}Z\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W
= Z\,\mathrm{d}W - Z\theta\,\mathrm{d}t + \tilde W Z\theta\,\mathrm{d}W + Z\theta\,\mathrm{d}t
= Z\big(1 + \theta\tilde W\big)\mathrm{d}W .
$$

The $\mathrm{d}t$ terms cancel, so $Z\tilde W$ is a stochastic integral, hence a local martingale.

**Step 3.** $\tilde W$ is continuous, starts at $0$, is a $\mathbb{Q}$-local martingale, and has
$[\tilde W]_t = t$. Lévy's characterisation makes it a $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

Now the statement that is actually used in papers.

<Corollary name="Change of drift for SDEs" label="Cor 5">
Let $X$ solve $\mathrm{d}X_t = b(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ under $\mathbb{P}$, with
$g(t)>0$ scalar. Let $\tilde b$ be another drift and set
$\theta_t = \big(\tilde b(X_t,t) - b(X_t,t)\big)/g(t)$. If Novikov holds for $\theta$ and
$\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q} = Z_T\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}$, then under $\mathbb{Q}$ the *same* process $X$
solves

$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = \tilde b(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W_t
$$

with $\tilde W$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion. Consequently, writing $\mathbb{P}^{b}$ and
$\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}$ for the laws on path space $C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^d)$ of the two SDEs started
from the same $X_0$,

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{b}}(X)
= \exp\!\Big(\int_0^T\frac{(\tilde b - b)(X_t,t)^{\top}}{g(t)^2}\big(\mathrm{d}X_t - b(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t\big)
- \tfrac12\int_0^T\frac{\|\tilde b - b\|^2(X_t,t)}{g(t)^2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).
$$
</Corollary>

<Proof>
Substituting $\mathrm{d}W_t = \mathrm{d}\tilde W_t + \theta_t\,\mathrm{d}t$ into the $\mathbb{P}$-SDE
gives $\mathrm{d}X = b\,\mathrm{d}t + g\theta\,\mathrm{d}t + g\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W = \tilde b\,\mathrm{d}t + g\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W$.
So under $\mathbb{Q}$, $X$ is a weak solution of the $\tilde b$-SDE, and if that SDE has a unique
law (Lipschitz coefficients suffice), the law of $X$ under $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}$.
The density formula is $Z_T$ with $\theta$ substituted and $g\,\mathrm{d}W_t = \mathrm{d}X_t - b\,\mathrm{d}t$,
which expresses $Z_T$ as a functional of the path $X$ alone. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="KL divergence between path measures" label="Thm 6">
In the setting of Cor 5, if $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}}\int_0^T\|\theta_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t$ is finite, then

$$
\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}\,\big\|\,\mathbb{P}^{b}\big)
= \tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\tilde b}}\int_0^T\frac{\big\|\tilde b(X_t,t) - b(X_t,t)\big\|^2}{g(t)^2}\,\mathrm{d}t ,
$$

where the expectation is over paths of the SDE with drift $\tilde b$. If the two SDEs start from
different initial laws $\tilde p_0$ and $p_0$, add $\mathrm{KL}(\tilde p_0\,\|\,p_0)$ (Exercise 3).
</Theorem>

<Proof>
$\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{Q}\|\mathbb{P}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\log Z_T]$ with
$\log Z_T = \int_0^T\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}W - \tfrac12\int_0^T\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t$. Under
$\mathbb{Q}$ write $\mathrm{d}W = \mathrm{d}\tilde W + \theta\,\mathrm{d}t$:

$$
\log Z_T = \int_0^T\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}\tilde W + \int_0^T\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t - \tfrac12\int_0^T\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t
= \int_0^T\theta^{\top}\mathrm{d}\tilde W + \tfrac12\int_0^T\|\theta\|^2\mathrm{d}t .
$$

The first term is an Itô integral against the $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion $\tilde W$ with
square-integrable integrand, so its $\mathbb{Q}$-expectation is zero. What remains is
$\tfrac12\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\int\|\theta\|^2$, and $\theta = (\tilde b - b)/g$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Warning>
Three things people get wrong. (i) The expectation in Thm 6 is under the *first* argument of the
KL, the process with drift $\tilde b$. (ii) The formula needs the *same* diffusion coefficient
on both sides. If the $g$'s differ, the path measures are mutually singular (their quadratic
variations differ almost surely) and the KL is $+\infty$. (iii) It is a statement on a finite
horizon $[0,T]$; on $[0,\infty)$ even a constant drift makes the measures singular (Exercise 4).
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="Brownian motion with and without a constant drift">
Let $d = 1$, $T = 1$, and compare $\mathbb{P}$, under which $X_t = W_t$, with $\mathbb{Q}$, under
which $X$ should be a Brownian motion with drift $c = 1$. Here $b = 0$, $\tilde b = c$, $g = 1$,
so $\theta_t = c$ is constant and

$$
Z_1 = \exp\!\big(cW_1 - \tfrac12c^2\big) = \exp\!\big(W_1 - \tfrac12\big).
$$

**Novikov.** $\mathbb{E}\exp(\tfrac12\int_0^1 c^2\mathrm{d}t) = e^{1/2}$, finite.

**Mass one.** $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}Z_1 = e^{-1/2}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{W_1}] = e^{-1/2}e^{1/2} = 1$, using
$\mathbb{E}[e^{aZ}] = e^{a^2/2}$ for $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ ([Gaussian computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations)).

**Law of $X_1$ under $\mathbb{Q}$.** For bounded $h$,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[h(W_1)] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\big[h(W_1)\,e^{W_1 - 1/2}\big]
= \int h(x)\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{x^2}{2} + x - \frac12\Big)\mathrm{d}x
= \int h(x)\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x-1)^2}{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x ,
$$

since $-\tfrac12x^2 + x - \tfrac12 = -\tfrac12(x-1)^2$. So $W_1\sim\mathcal{N}(1,1)$ under $\mathbb{Q}$,
which is the marginal at time $1$ of $ct + \tilde W_t$ with $c = 1$, as Thm 3 predicts. On the
particular path with $W_1 = 1.2$, $Z_1 = e^{0.7}\approx 2.01$: this path is twice as likely under
the drifted model as under the driftless one. A path with $W_1 = -0.3$ has $Z_1 = e^{-0.8}\approx 0.45$.

**KL between the path measures.** Thm 6 gives
$\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{Q}\|\mathbb{P}) = \tfrac12\int_0^1 c^2\,\mathrm{d}t = \tfrac12 c^2 T = 0.5$ nats.
Cross-check directly: $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\log Z_1] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[cW_1] - \tfrac12c^2 = c\cdot cT - \tfrac12c^2 = \tfrac12$, since $W_1\sim\mathcal{N}(cT,T)$ under $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Data processing check.** The marginals at time $1$ are $\mathcal{N}(1,1)$ and $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, with
$\mathrm{KL} = \tfrac12(1-0)^2/1 = 0.5$. Data processing says
$\mathrm{KL}(\text{marginals})\le\mathrm{KL}(\text{paths})$, and here it is an equality. That is
right: $Z_1$ depends on the path only through $W_1$, so $W_1$ is a sufficient statistic for
telling the two models apart and the rest of the path carries no extra evidence. For a
state-dependent drift, such as OU versus Brownian motion (Exercise 2), the inequality is strict.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **Likelihood bounds for diffusion models.** In <Cite id="2101.09258" />, the true reverse SDE
  and the learned one (drift $f - g^2\nabla\log p_t$ versus $f - g^2 s_\theta$) share $g$. Thm 6 plus the
  chain rule of KL gives
  $\mathrm{KL}(\text{true paths}\,\|\,\text{model paths}) = \mathrm{KL}(p_T\|\pi) + \tfrac12\int_0^T g(t)^2\,\mathbb{E}\|s_\theta - \nabla\log p_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t$,
  and data processing to the time-$0$ marginal turns this into
  $\mathrm{KL}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\|p_\theta)\le$ the weighted score-matching loss. That is their
  Theorem 1; the weighting $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ is called likelihood weighting for this reason.
- **Schrödinger bridges.** The dynamic SB problem minimises $\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}\|\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{ref}})$
  over path measures with fixed endpoints. Thm 6 turns it into the stochastic control problem
  $\min\tfrac12\mathbb{E}\int\|u_t\|^2/g^2\,\mathrm{d}t$ over drifts $u$ added to the reference,
  which is how <Cite id="2106.01357" /> and the [Schrödinger bridge page](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge)
  state it. Each half-bridge in IPF is a KL projection computed with this formula.
- **RL fine-tuning of diffusion and flow policies.** When a Diffusion Policy or a flow-based policy
  like $\pi_0$ is fine-tuned by [KL-regularised RL](/concepts/kl-regularized-rl), the KL between
  the fine-tuned and the pretrained samplers (two reverse SDEs, same noise schedule) is exactly the
  integrated squared drift difference of Thm 6. Adjoint-matching and stochastic-control style
  fine-tuning methods take this as their starting point.
- **Time reversal and the Schrödinger system.** Doob's $h$-transform, which conditions a diffusion
  on its endpoint, is Girsanov with $\theta = g\nabla\log h$; see
  [time reversal of diffusions](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions) for the closely related
  score drift.
- **Importance sampling of rare events.** Simulating under a drift that pushes toward the event and
  reweighting by $1/Z_T$ is Girsanov used as a variance reduction device; the same trick appears
  in off-policy evaluation for continuous-time control.

## Exercises

<Exercise hint="Set Y = theta W - theta^2 t / 2 and apply Ito to exp(Y); this is Exercise 3 of the Ito page with theta constant.">
Verify Lemma 1 directly for constant $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$ and $d = 1$: compute
$\mathrm{d}\exp(\theta W_t - \tfrac12\theta^2 t)$ and confirm the $\mathrm{d}t$ term vanishes.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Thm 6 with b = 0, tilde-b = -theta x, g = 1; you need the second moment of OU started at 0, which is (1 - exp(-2 theta t)) / (2 theta).">
Let $\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{OU}}$ be the law on $[0,T]$ of $\mathrm{d}X = -\theta X\,\mathrm{d}t + \mathrm{d}W$,
$X_0 = 0$, and $\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{BM}}$ the law of Brownian motion. Show
$$
\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{OU}}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{BM}}\big)
= \frac{\theta}{4}\Big(T - \frac{1 - e^{-2\theta T}}{2\theta}\Big),
$$
and compare with the KL of the time-$T$ marginals $\mathcal{N}(0,\tfrac{1-e^{-2\theta T}}{2\theta})$
versus $\mathcal{N}(0,T)$ for $\theta = 1$, $T = 1$. Which is larger, and why must it be?
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Disintegrate each path measure as (law of X_0) times (law of the path given X_0), then use the chain rule of KL from the KL page.">
Prove the addendum to Thm 6: if the $\tilde b$-SDE starts from $\tilde p_0$ and the $b$-SDE from
$p_0$, then the KL between path measures is
$\mathrm{KL}(\tilde p_0\|p_0) + \tfrac12\mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|\tilde b - b\|^2/g^2\,\mathrm{d}t$.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Under P, W_t / t tends to 0 almost surely (strong law); under Q, it tends to c. Find an event with probability 1 under one measure and 0 under the other.">
Show that for $c\neq 0$ the laws of $W_t$ and of $ct + W_t$ on $[0,\infty)$ are mutually singular,
even though they are equivalent on every $[0,T]$. Conclude that the Novikov density $Z_T$ cannot
converge in $L^1$ as $T\to\infty$, and reconcile this with $\mathrm{KL} = \tfrac12c^2T\to\infty$.
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Øksendal Ch. 8.6 states Girsanov in the form of Cor 5 with the vector, matrix-$\sigma$ case and
proves it via Lévy's characterisation as above. Karatzas and Shreve Ch. 3.5 gives the full
treatment, including the proof of Novikov's condition and the weaker Kazamaki criterion. For the
path-space KL formula in the generality that Schrödinger-bridge papers use it (general
semimartingale reference measures, initial-law terms), see Léonard, *A survey of the Schrödinger
problem*, Section 2, and the appendix of <Cite id="2106.01357" />.
