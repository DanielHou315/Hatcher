## Why you need this

Song et al. 2021 showed that DDPM (Ho et al. 2020) and NCSN (Song and Ermon 2019) are two
discretisations of one continuous-time object: a forward SDE that destroys data, whose
time reversal, driven by the score, regenerates it. Reading any later diffusion paper
requires this vocabulary: "VP" and "VE" SDEs, perturbation kernels, the reverse SDE, the
probability-flow ODE (which is what DDIM, DPM-Solver, and the flow-matching samplers of
$\pi_0$ integrate), predictor–corrector sampling, and the likelihood weighting of Song,
Durkan, Murray and Ermon 2021. Diffusion Policy runs the DDPM sampler on action chunks; the
Schrödinger-bridge methods of De Bortoli et al. 2021 use the VP-SDE as their reference
process. This page assembles the pieces proved elsewhere on the site and works one
Gaussian instance end to end.

## Setup

Fix a horizon $T$ (papers use $T = 1$). The **forward SDE** is
$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = f(X_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_t,\qquad X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{data}},
$$
with $f$ affine in $x$ and $g$ scalar, so every marginal $p_t$ is a Gaussian smoothing of
$p_{\mathrm{data}}$. Two families cover the literature.

<Definition name="VE and VP SDEs">
*Variance exploding (VE):* $f = 0$, $g(t) = \sqrt{\mathrm{d}\sigma^2(t)/\mathrm{d}t}$ for an
increasing $\sigma(t)$. *Variance preserving (VP):* $f(x,t) = -\tfrac12\beta(t)x$,
$g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$ for a positive schedule $\beta$. The VP-SDE is a time-inhomogeneous
[Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou); NCSN is a discretised VE-SDE and
DDPM a discretised VP-SDE.
</Definition>

<Proposition name="Perturbation kernels" label="Prop 1">
Write $p_{0t}(x_t|x_0)$ for the law of $X_t$ given $X_0 = x_0$. Then
$$
\text{VE: } p_{0t} = \mathcal N\big(x_0,\;[\sigma^2(t)-\sigma^2(0)]\,I\big),
\qquad
\text{VP: } p_{0t} = \mathcal N\big(e^{-\frac12\int_0^t\beta}\,x_0,\;[1-e^{-\int_0^t\beta}]\,I\big).
$$
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Both SDEs are linear with additive noise, so $X_t\mid X_0$ is Gaussian and it suffices to
track mean and covariance. VE: $X_t = x_0 + \int_0^t g\,\mathrm{d}W_s$, a Gaussian with
variance $\int_0^t g^2\,\mathrm{d}s = \sigma^2(t)-\sigma^2(0)$ by the Itô isometry. VP: the
integrating factor $e^{\frac12\int_0^t\beta}$ (see the OU example on the
[Itô](/concepts/ito-lemma) page) gives
$X_t = e^{-\frac12\int_0^t\beta}x_0 + \int_0^t e^{-\frac12\int_s^t\beta}\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,\mathrm{d}W_s$,
whose variance is $\int_0^t\beta(s)e^{-\int_s^t\beta}\mathrm{d}s = 1-e^{-\int_0^t\beta}$.
$\blacksquare$
</Proof>

For VP, $\bar\alpha_t = e^{-\int_0^t\beta}$ recovers DDPM's
$q(x_t|x_0) = \mathcal N(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I)$. If $\int_0^T\beta$ is large,
$p_T\approx\mathcal N(0,I)$ regardless of the data: the prior $\pi$.

## The idea

Three statements, each proved on its own page and quoted here with hypotheses, plus one
new proof.

<Theorem name="Reverse-time SDE (Anderson 1982)" label="Thm 2">
Suppose $p_t$ is $C^1$ in $t$, $C^2$ in $x$, positive, and $\nabla\log p_t$ grows at most
linearly. Then the process $\bar X_t = X_{T-t}$, run from $\bar X_0\sim p_T$, is a diffusion
with the same marginals $p_{T-t}$, solving (in the original clock, $\mathrm{d}t<0$)
$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = \big[f(X_t,t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla\log p_t(X_t)\big]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_t ,
$$
with $\bar W$ a Brownian motion in reversed time.
</Theorem>

The derivation, from the Fokker–Planck equation forward and backward, is on the
[time reversal](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions) page. Sampling then means: draw
$X_T\sim\pi$, replace $\nabla\log p_t$ by a network $s_\theta(x,t)$, integrate backward.

<Theorem name="Probability-flow ODE" label="Thm 3">
Under the same hypotheses, the deterministic ODE
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = f(x,t) - \tfrac12 g(t)^2\,\nabla\log p_t(x)
$$
transports $p_s$ to $p_t$ for every $s,t$: if $x(s)\sim p_s$ then $x(t)\sim p_t$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
The forward SDE's marginals satisfy the [Fokker–Planck](/concepts/fokker-planck) equation
$$
\partial_t p_t = -\nabla\!\cdot(f\,p_t) + \tfrac12 g^2\,\Delta p_t .
$$
Write the Laplacian as a divergence of a flux: $\Delta p_t = \nabla\!\cdot(\nabla p_t) = \nabla\!\cdot(p_t\nabla\log p_t)$.
Then
$$
\partial_t p_t = -\nabla\!\cdot\Big(\big[f - \tfrac12 g^2\nabla\log p_t\big]\,p_t\Big),
$$
which is the [continuity equation](/concepts/continuity-equation) for the velocity field
$v(x,t) = f - \tfrac12 g^2\nabla\log p_t$. A density transported by the ODE $\dot x = v$
satisfies the continuity equation with the same $v$; uniqueness of solutions (for $v$
locally Lipschitz with linear growth, which the hypotheses give) shows the transported
density and $p_t$ agree. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

The reverse SDE and the ODE share every marginal; they differ in the joint law of a
trajectory. The ODE gives a deterministic bijection noise $\leftrightarrow$ data and, via
the instantaneous change of variables formula (continuity-equation page),
$\log p_0(x_0) = \log p_T(x_T) + \int_0^T\nabla\!\cdot v(x_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t$, an exact
likelihood.

**Training.** Replace $\nabla\log p_t$ by $s_\theta$ fitted with
[denoising score matching](/concepts/score-matching) at every $t$, using Prop 1 for the
conditional score:
$$
\theta^\star = \arg\min_\theta\;\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal U[0,T]}\,\lambda(t)\,
\mathbb{E}_{x_0}\mathbb{E}_{x_t|x_0}\Big\|s_\theta(x_t,t) - \nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t|x_0)\Big\|^2 .
$$
With $\lambda(t) = 1/\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\log p_{0t}\|^2$ (proportional to the kernel variance) this
is the $\epsilon$-loss of DDPM.

<Theorem name="Likelihood weighting (Song, Durkan, Murray, Ermon 2021), statement" label="Thm 4">
Let $p_0^\theta$ be the law at time $0$ of the reverse SDE of Thm 2 with $\nabla\log p_t$
replaced by $s_\theta$ and started from $\pi$. Under Novikov-type integrability,
$$
\operatorname{KL}\big(p_{\mathrm{data}}\,\|\,p_0^\theta\big)
\;\le\; \tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}_t\,g(t)^2\,\mathbb{E}_{x_t}\big\|s_\theta(x_t,t)-\nabla\log p_t(x_t)\big\|^2\cdot T
+ \operatorname{KL}(p_T\|\pi).
$$
So the score-matching objective with $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ upper-bounds the negative
log-likelihood (up to entropy of the data). The proof is the
[KL between path measures](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds) via
[Girsanov](/concepts/girsanov-theorem): two SDEs with the same diffusion coefficient and
drifts differing by $g^2(s_\theta - \nabla\log p_t)$ have path-KL equal to half the
time-integrated squared drift difference over $g^2$, and marginalising to the endpoint can
only lower KL (data processing).
</Theorem>

**Samplers.**

- *Predictor:* Euler–Maruyama on the reverse SDE with step $\Delta$ going backward,
  $x_{t-\Delta} = x_t - \big[f(x_t,t) - g(t)^2 s_\theta(x_t,t)\big]\Delta + g(t)\sqrt{\Delta}\,z$,
  $z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$; or any ODE solver on Thm 3.
- *Corrector:* a few Langevin steps at fixed $t$,
  $x\leftarrow x + \varepsilon\,s_\theta(x,t) + \sqrt{2\varepsilon}\,z$, which leave $p_t$
  invariant when $s_\theta = \nabla\log p_t$ (its Fokker–Planck operator annihilates $p_t$;
  Exercise 3). The corrector repairs discretisation error of the predictor without changing
  the target.

<Proposition name="DDPM ancestral sampling is Euler–Maruyama on the reverse VP-SDE" label="Prop 5">
Discretise the VP-SDE on $N$ steps with $\beta_i = \beta(t_i)\Delta$. The DDPM update
$x_{i-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_i}}\big(x_i + \beta_i\,s_\theta(x_i,i)\big) + \sqrt{\beta_i}\,z$
agrees with the Euler–Maruyama step of Thm 2 up to $O(\beta_i^2)$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
In units where the step is one, $f = -\tfrac12\beta_i x$ and $g^2 = \beta_i$, so the
predictor step reads
$x_{i-1} = x_i + \tfrac12\beta_i x_i + \beta_i s_\theta + \sqrt{\beta_i}\,z = (1+\tfrac12\beta_i)x_i + \beta_i s_\theta + \sqrt{\beta_i}\,z$.
DDPM's coefficient is $(1-\beta_i)^{-1/2} = 1 + \tfrac12\beta_i + O(\beta_i^2)$ on $x_i$ and
$\beta_i(1+O(\beta_i))$ on $s_\theta$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Warning>
Papers differ on the direction of time. Song et al. run $t$ from $T$ down to $0$ and write
$\mathrm{d}t<0$ in Thm 2; flow-matching papers run $t$ from $0$ (noise) to $1$ (data). When
you change the clock, the sign of the drift flips but the diffusion term does not. Also,
Thm 3 with $s_\theta$ in place of the true score no longer has $p_t$ as its marginals: the
ODE and SDE samplers then produce different distributions, and neither is exactly $p_0$.
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="Gaussian data through the VP-SDE, forward and back">
Data $p_0 = \mathcal N(1,\tfrac14)$, constant $\beta = 2$, horizon $T = 1$, $d = 1$.

*Forward.* By Prop 1, $X_t\mid X_0\sim\mathcal N(e^{-t}X_0,\,1-e^{-2t})$. Since $X_0$ is
Gaussian, $p_t = \mathcal N(m(t),v(t))$ with
$$
m(t) = e^{-t},\qquad v(t) = \tfrac14 e^{-2t} + 1 - e^{-2t} = 1 - \tfrac34 e^{-2t},
$$
which solve the OU moment ODEs $\dot m = -m$, $\dot v = -2v + 2$. At $T = 1$:
$m(1) = 0.3679$, $v(1) = 1 - 0.75\cdot0.1353 = 0.8985$. The prior mismatch is
$\operatorname{KL}(p_1\|\mathcal N(0,1)) = \tfrac12[0.8985 + 0.1353 - 1 - \ln 0.8985] = \tfrac12[0.0338+0.1070] = 0.0704$
nats; this is the $\operatorname{KL}(p_T\|\pi)$ term of Thm 4.

*Score.* $\nabla\log p_t(x) = -(x-m(t))/v(t)$ ([score of a Gaussian](/concepts/gaussian-computations)).

*Reverse SDE.* With $f = -x$, $g^2 = 2$, Thm 2 gives the backward-time drift
$-f + g^2\nabla\log p_t = x - 2(x-m)/v$. In reversed clock $\tau = 1-t$,
$$
\mathrm{d}Y_\tau = \Big[\big(1-\tfrac{2}{v}\big)Y_\tau + \tfrac{2m}{v}\Big]\mathrm{d}\tau + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_\tau,
\qquad Y_0\sim p_1 .
$$
Check that it really returns to $p_0$: the mean $M(\tau)$ and variance $V(\tau)$ of $Y$ obey
$\dot M = (1-\tfrac2v)M + \tfrac{2m}{v}$ and $\dot V = 2(1-\tfrac2v)V + 2$. Substituting
$M = m$ gives $\dot M = m - \tfrac{2m}{v}+\tfrac{2m}{v} = m$, and indeed
$\mathrm{d}m/\mathrm{d}\tau = -\dot m = m$. Substituting $V = v$ gives
$\dot V = 2v - 4 + 2 = 2v-2$, and $\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}\tau = -(-2v+2) = 2v-2$. Both ODEs
are satisfied, so the reverse SDE retraces the forward marginals exactly and lands on
$\mathcal N(1,\tfrac14)$ at $\tau = 1$.

*Probability-flow ODE.* Thm 3: $\dot x = -x + (x-m)/v$, linear, so it maps Gaussians to
Gaussians with $\dot M = -M + (M-m)/v$ and $\dot V = 2(-1+\tfrac1v)V$. With $M = m$, $V = v$:
$\dot M = -m$ and $\dot V = -2v+2$, the forward moment ODEs again. Same marginals, no noise.

*One Euler–Maruyama step by hand.* Step $\Delta = 0.1$ backward from $t = 1$, where
$m = 0.3679$, $v = 0.8985$:
$$
x_{0.9} = x_1 - \big[-x_1 - 2\,s(x_1)\big]\Delta + \sqrt{2\Delta}\,z
= \underbrace{\big(1+\Delta-\tfrac{2\Delta}{v}\big)}_{0.8774}x_1 + \underbrace{\tfrac{2\Delta m}{v}}_{0.0819} + \sqrt{0.2}\,z .
$$
Starting from $x_1\sim p_1$ exactly, the new mean is $0.8774\cdot0.3679 + 0.0819 = 0.4047$
and the new variance $0.8774^2\cdot0.8985 + 0.2 = 0.6917 + 0.2 = 0.8917$. The true
$p_{0.9}$ has $m = e^{-0.9} = 0.4066$ and $v = 1-0.75e^{-1.8} = 0.8760$. Errors: $0.002$ in
the mean, $0.016$ in the variance, both $O(\Delta)$ as expected. Redo it with $\Delta = 0.5$:
coefficient $0.3870$, offset $0.4094$, noise variance $1$, giving variance $1.135$ against
the true $0.724$; a single coarse step overshoots badly, which is why DDPM needs hundreds
of steps and why the corrector exists.

*The same step on the ODE.* $x_{0.9} = x_1 + \Delta\big[x_1 + s(x_1)\big] = 0.9887\,x_1 + 0.0409$:
mean $0.4047$, variance $0.9887^2\cdot0.8985 = 0.8783$. The variance error drops to $0.002$
because no noise is injected and then re-contracted; this is the practical reason ODE
samplers take fewer steps.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **Score SDE** <Cite id="2011.13456" />: eq. (5) forward SDE, eq. (6) reverse SDE, eq. (7)
  training objective, eq. (13) probability-flow ODE, Appendix E for Prop 5 and
  predictor–corrector; the [paper page](/papers/score-sde-song-2021) decomposes it.
- **DDPM** <Cite id="2006.11239" />: eq. (4) is Prop 1 (VP), Algorithm 2 is Prop 5. The
  $\sigma_t^2 = \beta_t$ choice is the Euler–Maruyama noise scale.
- **Likelihood weighting** <Cite id="2101.09258" />: Theorems 1–2 there are Thm 4; setting
  $\lambda = g^2$ turns diffusion training into (bounded) maximum likelihood.
- **EDM** <Cite id="2206.00364" />: re-parametrises VE with $\sigma$ as the clock, so the
  ODE of Thm 3 becomes $\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}\sigma = -\sigma\nabla\log p_\sigma(x)$ and
  Heun steps replace Euler; all samplers there are Thm 2 and Thm 3 in different coordinates.
- **Diffusion Policy** (Chi et al. 2023) samples action chunks with DDPM (Prop 5) or DDIM
  (Thm 3, with $\hat x_0$ from Tweedie). **$\pi_0$** (Black et al. 2024) integrates a
  [flow-matching](/concepts/flow-matching) ODE, which is Thm 3 in the noise-to-data clock.
- **Diffusion Schrödinger bridge** (De Bortoli et al. 2021): the reference process is the
  VP-SDE, and each half-bridge is a time reversal of the previous one (Thm 2 with a learned
  drift); see [Schrödinger bridge](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge).

## Exercises

<Exercise hint="Use the Ito isometry on the stochastic integral and the geometric ladder sigma(t) = sigma_min (sigma_max / sigma_min)^t.">
For the VE-SDE with $\sigma(t) = \sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t$, compute
$g(t)$ and the perturbation kernel of Prop 1, and show $p_T\approx\mathcal N(x_0,\sigma_{\max}^2I)$
is not data-independent. What prior do NCSN-style samplers use instead, and what does that
cost in Thm 4?
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="The ODE is linear in x with time-dependent coefficients; solve for x(t) given x(1), then compute mean and variance of the image of N(m(1), v(1)).">
In the worked example, solve Thm 3's ODE exactly from $t = 1$ to $t = 0$ and verify that it
maps $\mathcal N(m(1),v(1))$ onto $\mathcal N(1,\tfrac14)$. Where does the trajectory
starting at $x_1 = m(1)$ end?
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Write the Fokker–Planck equation of the Langevin SDE and substitute p_t; the drift and diffusion terms cancel identically.">
Show that $\mathrm{d}x = \nabla\log p_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_s$ (with $t$
frozen) has $p_t$ as a stationary density, and deduce that the corrector step leaves
$p_t$ invariant in the limit of small $\varepsilon$.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Path KL = (1/2) integral of |drift difference|^2 / g^2; the drift difference is g^2 (s_theta - grad log p_t).">
Using the path-measure KL formula on the [KL page](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds),
derive the integrand of Thm 4 and explain where the factor $g(t)^2$ comes from. Why does
the VP schedule with constant $\beta$ make Thm 4 and the DDPM $\epsilon$-loss differ only
by the weighting $\beta/(1-\bar\alpha_t)$?
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Song et al. 2021, Appendices A–E, contain every derivation quoted here; read them after the
[time reversal](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions) and
[Fokker–Planck](/concepts/fokker-planck) pages. Anderson 1982 is the original reversal
theorem with its measurability hypotheses. Karras et al. 2022 is the best guide to the
design choices (noise schedule, preconditioning, solver) once the framework is understood.
