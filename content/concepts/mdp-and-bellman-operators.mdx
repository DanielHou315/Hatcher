## Why you need this

Almost every reinforcement learning algorithm is a noisy fixed-point iteration for one of two
maps on value functions. DQN regresses onto $r + \gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')$, which is one sample
of the Bellman optimality operator; SAC <Cite id="1801.01290" /> regresses onto a *soft*
version of the same thing; PPO's advantage estimator is built from the residual
$r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$ of the Bellman expectation operator. When DDPO
<Cite id="2305.13301" /> and DPPO <Cite id="2409.00588" /> fine-tune a diffusion model or a
diffusion policy with RL, the first thing they do is declare the denoising chain to be an MDP
so that all of this machinery applies. The page proves the two facts everything rests on: the
Bellman operators are $\gamma$-contractions, and acting greedily with respect to a value
function never makes a policy worse.

## Setup

<Definition name="Markov decision process">
A (finite, discounted) MDP is a tuple $(\mathcal S,\mathcal A,P,r,\gamma)$: a finite state set
$\mathcal S$, a finite action set $\mathcal A$, transition probabilities $P(s'\mid s,a)$, a
bounded reward $r(s,a)\in[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]$, and a discount $\gamma\in[0,1)$. A (stationary)
policy is a conditional distribution $\pi(a\mid s)$. Running $\pi$ from $s_0=s$ produces
$a_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s_t)$, $s_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$, and the value functions

$$
V^\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t\ge 0}\gamma^t r(s_t,a_t)\,\Big|\,s_0 = s\Big],
\qquad
Q^\pi(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\,V^\pi(s').
$$

The series converges absolutely because $|r|\le R_{\max}$, so $|V^\pi|\le R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)$.
</Definition>

We treat $V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal S|}$ as a vector and use the sup norm
$\|V\|_\infty=\max_s|V(s)|$ and the componentwise order $V\le W$. For a policy $\pi$ write

$$
r_\pi(s) = \sum_a \pi(a\mid s)\,r(s,a),
\qquad
P_\pi(s'\mid s) = \sum_a \pi(a\mid s)\,P(s'\mid s,a),
$$

so $P_\pi$ is a row-stochastic matrix (nonnegative entries, rows summing to one) and
$(P_\pi V)(s) = \sum_{s'}P_\pi(s'\mid s)V(s')$ is the expected next value.

<Definition name="Bellman operators">
The *expectation* operator of $\pi$ and the *optimality* operator are the maps
$T^\pi, T:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal S|}\to\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal S|}$,

$$
T^\pi V = r_\pi + \gamma P_\pi V,
\qquad
(TV)(s) = \max_{a}\Big[r(s,a) + \gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\,V(s')\Big].
$$

The same operators act on $Q$-functions: $(TQ)(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\max_{a'}Q(s',a')$.
</Definition>

<Proposition name="Bellman equation">
$V^\pi = T^\pi V^\pi$, and therefore $V^\pi = (I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1} r_\pi$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Split off the first reward. Conditional on $(s_0,a_0,s_1)$ the remaining sum
$\sum_{t\ge1}\gamma^t r(s_t,a_t)$ has expectation $\gamma V^\pi(s_1)$ because the process
started at $s_1$ is again the process started at a state, run under $\pi$ (the Markov property).
Averaging over $a_0\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)$ and $s_1\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a_0)$ gives
$V^\pi(s) = r_\pi(s) + \gamma(P_\pi V^\pi)(s)$. For the matrix form, the induced sup norm of
$P_\pi$ is its maximal row sum, $1$, so $\|\gamma P_\pi\|_\infty = \gamma$ and the Neumann series
$(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1} = \sum_{k\ge0}\gamma^k P_\pi^k$ converges (see
[matrix calculus](/concepts/matrix-calculus)). $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

## The idea

Both operators have two elementary properties, and everything else is a consequence of them.

<Lemma name="Monotone and translation-by-gamma">
For $F\in\{T^\pi, T\}$, any $V,W$, and any constant $c\in\mathbb{R}$ (with $\mathbf 1$ the
all-ones vector):

1. Monotone: $V\le W$ implies $FV\le FW$.
2. Translation: $F(V + c\mathbf 1) = FV + \gamma c\mathbf 1$.
</Lemma>

<Proof>
For $T^\pi$: $P_\pi$ has nonnegative entries, so $P_\pi V\le P_\pi W$; and $P_\pi\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$.
For $T$: each bracket $r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V(s')$ is monotone in $V$ and shifts by
$\gamma c$ when $V$ shifts by $c$; a maximum of monotone functions is monotone, and a maximum of
functions all shifted by $\gamma c$ shifts by $\gamma c$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Bellman operators are gamma-contractions" label="Thm 1">
For $F\in\{T^\pi,T\}$ and all $V,W$: $\|FV - FW\|_\infty \le \gamma\,\|V-W\|_\infty$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Let $c = \|V-W\|_\infty$. Then $W - c\mathbf 1\le V\le W + c\mathbf 1$ componentwise. Apply $F$
and use the lemma twice:
$FW - \gamma c\mathbf 1 \le FV \le FW + \gamma c\mathbf 1$, i.e. $|FV - FW|\le\gamma c$ in every
component. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

A contraction on a complete space has exactly one fixed point, and iterating the map finds it.
Completeness means every Cauchy sequence converges; $\mathbb{R}^n$ with any norm is complete,
which is all we need here.

<Theorem name="Banach fixed-point theorem" label="Thm 2">
Let $(X,d)$ be a complete metric space and $F:X\to X$ satisfy $d(Fx,Fy)\le\gamma\,d(x,y)$ with
$\gamma\in[0,1)$. Then $F$ has a unique fixed point $x^\star$, and for every $x_0$ the iterates
$x_{k+1}=Fx_k$ satisfy

$$
d(x_k,x^\star)\le\gamma^k d(x_0,x^\star)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d(x_k,x^\star)\le\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\,d(x_k,x_{k-1}).
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Since $d(x_{k+1},x_k) = d(Fx_k,Fx_{k-1})\le\gamma\,d(x_k,x_{k-1})\le\gamma^k d(x_1,x_0)$, for
$m>k$ the triangle inequality gives
$d(x_m,x_k)\le\sum_{j=k}^{m-1}\gamma^j d(x_1,x_0)\le\frac{\gamma^k}{1-\gamma}d(x_1,x_0)\to0$,
so $(x_k)$ is Cauchy and converges to some $x^\star$. $F$ is Lipschitz, hence continuous, so
$Fx^\star=\lim Fx_k=\lim x_{k+1}=x^\star$. If $y^\star$ is another fixed point then
$d(x^\star,y^\star)=d(Fx^\star,Fy^\star)\le\gamma\,d(x^\star,y^\star)$ forces
$d(x^\star,y^\star)=0$. The first rate is $d(F^kx_0,F^kx^\star)\le\gamma^k d(x_0,x^\star)$. For
the second, $d(x_k,x^\star)\le d(x_k,x_{k+1})+d(x_{k+1},x^\star)\le\gamma\,d(x_{k-1},x_k)+\gamma\,d(x_k,x^\star)$;
rearrange. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Corollary name="Value iteration">
$V^\pi$ is the unique fixed point of $T^\pi$. $T$ has a unique fixed point, written $V^\star$,
and value iteration $V_{k+1}=TV_k$ from any $V_0$ satisfies
$\|V_k - V^\star\|_\infty\le\gamma^k\|V_0-V^\star\|_\infty$.
</Corollary>

It remains to show that $V^\star$ deserves its name.

<Theorem name="Optimality" label="Thm 3">
$V^\pi\le V^\star$ for every policy $\pi$. If $\pi^\star$ is greedy with respect to $V^\star$,
meaning $\pi^\star(s)\in\arg\max_a\big[r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V^\star(s')\big]$, then
$V^{\pi^\star}=V^\star$. In particular an optimal policy exists and can be taken deterministic
and stationary.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
A weighted average is at most the maximum, so $T^\pi V\le TV$ for every $V$ and $\pi$. Hence
$V^\pi = T^\pi V^\pi\le TV^\pi$. Apply the monotone map $T$ repeatedly:
$V^\pi\le TV^\pi\le T^2V^\pi\le\cdots\le T^kV^\pi\to V^\star$. For the greedy policy the
maximum in $T$ is attained by $\pi^\star$, so $T^{\pi^\star}V^\star = TV^\star = V^\star$; thus
$V^\star$ is a fixed point of $T^{\pi^\star}$, and by uniqueness $V^\star=V^{\pi^\star}$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Policy improvement" label="Thm 4">
Let $\pi'$ be greedy with respect to $Q^\pi$, i.e. $\pi'(s)\in\arg\max_a Q^\pi(s,a)$. Then
$V^{\pi'}\ge V^\pi$, and if $V^{\pi'}=V^\pi$ then $\pi$ is optimal.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Greediness says $T^{\pi'}V^\pi = TV^\pi\ge T^\pi V^\pi = V^\pi$. By monotonicity of $T^{\pi'}$,
$V^\pi\le T^{\pi'}V^\pi\le(T^{\pi'})^2V^\pi\le\cdots\to V^{\pi'}$. If equality holds
throughout then $TV^\pi = V^\pi$, so $V^\pi$ is the fixed point of $T$, i.e. $V^\pi=V^\star$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Intuition>
The contraction says one Bellman backup removes a fraction $1-\gamma$ of the error, uniformly
over states, because the only place the current guess $V$ enters is through one step of
lookahead weighted by $\gamma$. Policy improvement says: if a one-step deviation to $\pi'$
followed by $\pi$ is no worse than $\pi$, then deviating for two steps is no worse than
deviating for one, and so on forever.
</Intuition>

<Warning>
Every statement above used $\gamma$ strictly less than $1$. With $\gamma=1$ (undiscounted,
episodic) $T$ is merely nonexpansive and fixed points need not be unique; convergence then
needs a termination argument (proper policies). For infinite state spaces the same proofs go
through on the space of bounded functions with the sup norm, which is still complete; for
continuous actions replace $\max$ by $\sup$ and you additionally need a measurable selection
of near-greedy actions.
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="Value iteration on a three-state MDP">
States $\{1,2,3\}$, actions $\{\mathrm{stay},\mathrm{go}\}$, $\gamma=0.9$:

- State 1. stay: $r=0.2$, stays in 1. go: $r=0$, moves to 2.
- State 2. stay: $r=0$, stays in 2. go: $r=0$, moves to 3 or to 1 with probability $\tfrac12$ each.
- State 3. stay: $r=1$, stays in 3 or drops to 1 with probability $\tfrac12$ each. go: $r=0.5$, stays in 3.

State 1 trades a sure small reward against a gamble on reaching state 3; state 3 trades a
larger reward against the risk of falling back to 1. Start value iteration from $V_0=(0,0,0)$
and write $Q_k(s,a) = r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V_{k-1}(s')$.

Iteration 1 (only rewards matter since $V_0=0$): $Q_1(1,\cdot)=(0.2,0)$, $Q_1(2,\cdot)=(0,0)$,
$Q_1(3,\cdot)=(1,0.5)$, so $V_1=(0.2,\,0,\,1)$.

Iteration 2:

- $Q_2(1,\mathrm{stay}) = 0.2+0.9\cdot0.2 = 0.38$; $Q_2(1,\mathrm{go}) = 0.9\cdot0 = 0$.
- $Q_2(2,\mathrm{stay}) = 0$; $Q_2(2,\mathrm{go}) = 0.9\,(0.5\cdot1+0.5\cdot0.2) = 0.54$.
- $Q_2(3,\mathrm{stay}) = 1+0.9\,(0.5\cdot1+0.5\cdot0.2) = 1.54$; $Q_2(3,\mathrm{go}) = 0.5+0.9\cdot1 = 1.4$.

So $V_2=(0.38,\,0.54,\,1.54)$, greedy actions (stay, go, stay).

Iteration 3:

- $Q_3(1,\mathrm{stay}) = 0.2+0.9\cdot0.38 = 0.542$; $Q_3(1,\mathrm{go}) = 0.9\cdot0.54 = 0.486$.
- $Q_3(2,\mathrm{stay}) = 0.9\cdot0.54 = 0.486$; $Q_3(2,\mathrm{go}) = 0.9\,(0.5\cdot1.54+0.5\cdot0.38) = 0.864$.
- $Q_3(3,\mathrm{stay}) = 1+0.9\cdot0.96 = 1.864$; $Q_3(3,\mathrm{go}) = 0.5+0.9\cdot1.54 = 1.886$.

So $V_3=(0.542,\,0.864,\,1.886)$ and the greedy action at state 3 has flipped to go: once
$V(3)$ is large enough, giving up half the reward to avoid the fall to state 1 pays.

**Exact answer.** Guess that go is optimal everywhere and evaluate that policy with the Bellman
equation. State 3 is decoupled: $V(3) = 0.5+0.9V(3)$, so $V(3)=5$. Then
$V(2) = 0.9\,(0.5\cdot5+0.5V(1)) = 2.25+0.45V(1)$ and $V(1)=0.9V(2)$, giving
$V(1) = 2.025+0.405V(1)$, i.e. $V(1)=2.025/0.595=405/119\approx3.4034$ and
$V(2)=450/119\approx3.7815$. Check greediness (Thm 4): at state 1, stay gives
$0.2+0.9\cdot3.4034=3.263$ versus go $3.4034$; at state 2, stay gives $3.403$ versus go $3.7815$;
at state 3, stay gives $1+0.9\,(2.5+1.7017)=4.7815$ versus go $5$. Go wins everywhere, so
$V^\star=(3.4034,\,3.7815,\,5)$ and $\pi^\star=$ go everywhere.

**Sanity checks.** The a priori bound of Thm 2 gives
$\|V_3-V^\star\|_\infty\le0.9^3\cdot\|V_0-V^\star\|_\infty = 0.729\cdot5=3.645$; the actual error
is $\max(2.861,\,2.918,\,3.114)=3.114$. The a posteriori bound gives
$\tfrac{0.9}{0.1}\|V_3-V_2\|_\infty = 9\cdot0.346 = 3.114$, exactly the true error. That is not
luck: at state 3 the optimal action is a self-loop, so the map $V(3)\mapsto0.5+0.9V(3)$ is an
affine contraction with factor exactly $\gamma$, and the a posteriori bound is tight for such
maps. Finally, since $V_0=0\le TV_0=V_1$, monotonicity gives $V_0\le V_1\le V_2\le\cdots$, which
the numbers confirm.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **DQN.** The target $y = r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\theta^-}(s',a')$ is $(TQ_{\theta^-})(s,a)$ evaluated
  with one sample of $s'$; the squared loss is a sampled Bellman residual. Whether the noisy
  iteration converges is the subject of
  [stochastic approximation and TD](/concepts/stochastic-approximation-and-td).
- **SAC** <Cite id="1801.01290" />, eqs. (2)–(3): the soft Bellman backup
  $T^\pi Q = r+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{s'}[V(s')]$ with $V(s)=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q(s,a)-\alpha\log\pi(a\mid s)]$
  is the expectation operator of an entropy-regularized MDP; its contraction proof is the one
  above with an extra bounded term. See [KL-regularized RL](/concepts/kl-regularized-rl).
- **PPO and GAE.** The TD residual $\delta_t=r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)$ is a one-sample
  estimate of $(T^\pi V-V)(s_t)$; it has conditional mean zero exactly when $V=V^\pi$. The
  policy update itself is justified by the
  [policy gradient theorem](/concepts/policy-gradient-theorem), whose proof uses
  $V^\pi=(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi$ in differentiated form.
- **RL for diffusion models and policies.** DDPO <Cite id="2305.13301" /> and DPPO
  <Cite id="2409.00588" /> define an MDP whose state is $(x_t,t,c)$, whose action is the next
  denoised sample $x_{t-1}$, and whose reward is paid only at $x_0$. The transition is
  deterministic given the action, so the Bellman equation collapses to
  $Q(x_t,t,x_{t-1}) = \mathbb{E}[r(x_0)\mid x_{t-1}]$, and PPO can be run on it. The same trick
  is what lets one talk about the "value" of a partially denoised action in Diffusion Policy.

## Exercises

<Exercise hint="Use the inequality |max f − max g| bounded by max |f − g| on the inner max over a', then the outer average over s'.">
Show that $T$ acting on $Q$-functions, $(TQ)(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\max_{a'}Q(s',a')$,
is a $\gamma$-contraction in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Write the 3-by-3 system (I − 0.9 P)V = r for the policy go-everywhere; the third row is decoupled and gives V(3) = 5 immediately.">
Verify $V^\pi=(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi$ numerically for the policy "go everywhere" in the
worked example by writing out $P_\pi$ and $r_\pi$ and solving the linear system. You should
recover $(405/119,\,450/119,\,5)$.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Evaluate stay-everywhere first: V(1) = 2, V(2) = 0, V(3) = 1.9/0.55. Then improve, evaluate, improve.">
Run policy iteration on the worked example starting from "stay everywhere". Show that two
improvement steps reach the optimal policy and that a third confirms it (Thm 4).
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Monotonicity plus translation: apply F to V − c1 ≤ W ≤ V + c1 exactly as in the proof of Thm 1.">
Let $F$ be any monotone map with $F(V+c\mathbf 1)=FV+\gamma c\mathbf 1$ for all constants $c$.
Prove $F$ is a $\gamma$-contraction in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. (This is the lemma that makes the
soft Bellman operator of SAC a contraction without redoing the proof.)
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Puterman Ch. 6 does everything above for countable state spaces and general bounded rewards,
including the sup-norm setting hinted at in the warning, and proves that policy iteration
terminates in finitely many steps for finite MDPs. Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis Ch. 2 organize the
whole theory around the two lemma properties (monotonicity and constant-shift), which is the
cleanest route to the regularized and stochastic versions used later on this site. Sutton and
Barto Ch. 4 is the place for the algorithmic view (policy iteration, asynchronous backups).
