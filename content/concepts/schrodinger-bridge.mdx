## Why you need this

A diffusion model runs a fixed noising SDE for long enough that the terminal law is
approximately Gaussian, then learns to reverse it. The reversal is exact only if the
terminal law was exactly Gaussian, which it never is, and the horizon has to be long for the
error to be small. The Schrödinger bridge asks for the diffusion that starts *exactly* at the
data distribution and ends *exactly* at the Gaussian (or at any other distribution, e.g. a
second image domain, a second robot trajectory set) in finite time while staying as close as
possible to a reference Brownian motion. Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (De Bortoli et al. 2021)
solves it by iterating time reversals; Chen, Liu and Theodorou (2022) train it by a
forward–backward SDE likelihood; Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching (Shi et al. 2023) and
$\mathrm{I}^2\mathrm{SB}$ (Liu et al. 2023) are the current practical forms. Every one of these
uses the four facts on this page: the bridge is entropic OT, its optimal coupling factorizes
through two potentials, its dynamics are an SDE with drift $\sigma^2\nabla\log\varphi$, and
the natural algorithm alternates two half bridges.

## Setup

Paths live in $\Omega = C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^d)$ and $X_t$ denotes the coordinate at time $t$. A
*path measure* is a probability measure on $\Omega$, e.g. the law of an SDE solution. For a
path measure $P$ write $P_t$ for the law of $X_t$, $P_{01}$ for the joint law of $(X_0,X_1)$,
and $P^{xy}$ for the conditional law of the path given $X_0 = x$, $X_1 = y$ (the *bridge* of
$P$). By disintegration, $P = \int P^{xy}\,P_{01}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)$.

The *reference* is $R$, the law of $\mathrm{d}X_t = \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ with $X_0\sim m$,
where $m$ is any initial law with $\mu_0\ll m$; we take $m = \mu_0$ for definiteness. Its
transition density is the heat kernel
$r_t(x,y) = (2\pi\sigma^2t)^{-d/2}\exp\big(-\|y-x\|^2/(2\sigma^2t)\big)$, so
$R_{01}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y) = m(x)\,r_1(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y$, and its bridges
$R^{xy}$ are Brownian bridges: under $R^{xy}$,
$X_t \sim \mathcal N\big((1-t)x + ty,\ \sigma^2t(1-t)I\big)$ with density
$r_t(x,z)\,r_{1-t}(z,y)/r_1(x,y)$ in $z$.

KL between path measures is the object from the [Girsanov page](/concepts/girsanov-theorem):
for two diffusions with the same $\sigma$ and drifts $b^P$, $b^R$,
$\mathrm{KL}(P\|R) = \mathrm{KL}(P_0\|R_0) + \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\mathbb{E}_P\int_0^1\|b^P_t - b^R_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t$.

<Definition name="Dynamic Schrödinger bridge">
Given $\mu_0,\mu_1$ with finite second moments and finite entropy,
$$
P^\star = \operatorname*{arg\,min}\big\{\mathrm{KL}(P\,\|\,R) : P \text{ a path measure},\ P_0 = \mu_0,\ P_1 = \mu_1\big\}.
$$
</Definition>

Schrödinger's 1931 question was: a cloud of Brownian particles is observed to have
empirical law $\mu_0$ at time $0$ and $\mu_1$ at time $1$, which is not what diffusion would
predict; what is the most likely way it got there? By Sanov's theorem the answer is the
path measure closest to $R$ in KL, which is the definition.

## The idea

<Theorem name="Static reduction: the bridge is entropic OT" label="Thm 1">
The minimizer exists, is unique, and has the form
$$
P^\star = \int R^{xy}\,\pi^\star(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y),
\qquad
\pi^\star = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)}\mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,R_{01}).
$$
Moreover, for $\pi$ with a density,
$$
\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|R_{01}) = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\Big[\int\tfrac12\|x-y\|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi + \sigma^2\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\mu_0\otimes\mu_1)\Big] + \text{const},
$$
so $\pi^\star$ solves the entropic OT problem with cost $\tfrac12\|x-y\|^2$ and $\varepsilon = \sigma^2$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
*Chain rule.* For any path measure $P$ with $P\ll R$, disintegrating both measures over the
endpoints gives (this is the chain rule of KL from the
[KL page](/concepts/kl-divergence-and-variational-bounds), applied to the map
$\omega\mapsto(X_0,X_1)$)
$$
\mathrm{KL}(P\|R) = \mathrm{KL}(P_{01}\|R_{01}) + \int\mathrm{KL}(P^{xy}\|R^{xy})\,P_{01}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y).
$$
The constraint $P_0 = \mu_0$, $P_1 = \mu_1$ only touches $P_{01}$, which must lie in
$\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)$; the second term is nonnegative and zero iff $P^{xy} = R^{xy}$ for
$P_{01}$-a.e. $(x,y)$. So the minimum is attained by choosing $P^{xy} = R^{xy}$ and $P_{01} = \pi^\star$.
Existence and uniqueness of $\pi^\star$ are the continuous version of Prop 1 on the entropic
OT page (strict convexity of KL plus a compactness argument; Léonard, Thm 2.12).

*Constants.* With $m = \mu_0$,
$$
\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|R_{01}) = \int\log\pi\,\mathrm{d}\pi - \int\log\mu_0\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0
+ \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\int\|x-y\|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi + \frac d2\log(2\pi\sigma^2),
$$
and $\int\log\pi\,\mathrm{d}\pi = \mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\mu_0\otimes\mu_1) + \int\log\mu_0\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0 +
\int\log\mu_1\,\mathrm{d}\mu_1$. Collect the $\pi$-dependent terms and factor $1/\sigma^2$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

So the *static* problem is the last page's problem with $K(x,y) = e^{-\|x-y\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}$,
and by the continuous version of the diagonal-scaling structure (Prop 1 there, Csiszár 1975
in general), $\pi^\star$ factorizes:

<Theorem name="The Schrödinger system" label="Thm 2">
There exist nonnegative functions $\hat\varphi_0$, $\varphi_1$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ such that
$$
\pi^\star(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y) = \hat\varphi_0(x)\,r_1(x,y)\,\varphi_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y .
$$
Define the *forward* and *backward* potentials by running the heat semigroup,
$$
\hat\varphi_t(z) = \int\hat\varphi_0(x)\,r_t(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad
\varphi_t(z) = \int r_{1-t}(z,y)\,\varphi_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}y ,
$$
so that $\partial_t\hat\varphi = \tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta\hat\varphi$ (forward heat) and
$\partial_t\varphi + \tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta\varphi = 0$ (backward heat). Then the marginals of
the bridge are
$$
P^\star_t(z) = \varphi_t(z)\,\hat\varphi_t(z)\qquad\text{for all } t\in[0,1],
$$
and in particular $\mu_0 = \varphi_0\hat\varphi_0$ and $\mu_1 = \varphi_1\hat\varphi_1$, which
is the *Schrödinger system*: two coupled equations for the two unknown functions $\hat\varphi_0$, $\varphi_1$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
The factorization is the continuous analogue of $P = \operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$
with $u\leftrightarrow\hat\varphi_0$, $v\leftrightarrow\varphi_1$; the constant $m(x)$ from
$R_{01}$ is absorbed into $\hat\varphi_0$. For the marginals, use Thm 1 and the Brownian
bridge density:
$$
P^\star_t(z) = \iint\hat\varphi_0(x)\,r_1(x,y)\,\varphi_1(y)\cdot\frac{r_t(x,z)\,r_{1-t}(z,y)}{r_1(x,y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y
= \Big[\int\hat\varphi_0(x)r_t(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]\Big[\int r_{1-t}(z,y)\varphi_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big]
= \hat\varphi_t(z)\,\varphi_t(z).
$$
The heat equations are the standard properties of the heat kernel. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="The bridge is an SDE with drift the log-gradient of the backward potential" label="Thm 3">
$P^\star$ is the law of the solution of
$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = \sigma^2\,\nabla\log\varphi_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t,\qquad X_0\sim\mu_0 .
$$
Equivalently, running time backwards from $X_1\sim\mu_1$,
$\mathrm{d}X_t = -\sigma^2\nabla\log\hat\varphi_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}\bar W_t$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
*The density process.* From Thm 1, $\mathrm{d}P^\star/\mathrm{d}R = \pi^\star(X_0,X_1)/R_{01}(X_0,X_1)
= \hat\varphi_0(X_0)\varphi_1(X_1)/m(X_0)$. Condition on $\mathcal F_t$ under $R$: by the
Markov property of Brownian motion,
$\mathbb{E}_R[\varphi_1(X_1)\mid\mathcal F_t] = \int r_{1-t}(X_t,y)\varphi_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}y = \varphi_t(X_t)$, so
$$
Z_t := \frac{\mathrm{d}P^\star}{\mathrm{d}R}\Big|_{\mathcal F_t} = \frac{\hat\varphi_0(X_0)}{m(X_0)}\,\varphi_t(X_t),
\qquad Z_0 = \frac{\hat\varphi_0\varphi_0(X_0)}{m(X_0)} = \frac{\mu_0(X_0)}{m(X_0)} .
$$

*Itô on the potential.* Under $R$, $\mathrm{d}X_t = \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t$. For $t<1$ the
function $\varphi_t$ is smooth and strictly positive (a heat-flow image of a nonnegative,
nonzero function). Itô's lemma and the backward heat equation give
$$
\mathrm{d}\varphi_t(X_t) = \big(\partial_t\varphi + \tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta\varphi\big)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathrm{d}W_t
= \sigma\,\varphi_t(X_t)\,\nabla\log\varphi_t(X_t)\cdot\mathrm{d}W_t ,
$$
so $\varphi_t(X_t)$ is a positive local martingale and, applying Itô to $\log$,
$$
\varphi_t(X_t) = \varphi_0(X_0)\exp\Big(\int_0^t\sigma\nabla\log\varphi_s(X_s)\cdot\mathrm{d}W_s
- \tfrac12\int_0^t\sigma^2\|\nabla\log\varphi_s(X_s)\|^2\mathrm{d}s\Big).
$$

*Girsanov.* The exponential is the Girsanov density for changing the drift of
$\mathrm{d}X = \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W$ to $b_t = \sigma^2\nabla\log\varphi_t$, since
$b/\sigma = \sigma\nabla\log\varphi$. We do not need Novikov's condition: $Z_t$ is a true
martingale because it was constructed as a conditional expectation of the integrable
$Z_1$. By Girsanov's theorem, under $P^\star$ the process
$\tilde W_t = W_t - \int_0^t\sigma\nabla\log\varphi_s(X_s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ is a Brownian motion,
so $\mathrm{d}X_t = \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t = \sigma^2\nabla\log\varphi_t(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}\tilde W_t$,
with $X_0\sim\mu_0$ from the factor $Z_0$.

*Reverse form.* By the [time-reversal formula](/concepts/time-reversal-of-diffusions), the
reverse-time drift is the forward drift minus $\sigma^2\nabla\log P^\star_t$, and by Thm 2
$\sigma^2\nabla\log\varphi_t - \sigma^2\nabla\log(\varphi_t\hat\varphi_t) = -\sigma^2\nabla\log\hat\varphi_t$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Intuition>
Thm 3 is Doob's $h$-transform: conditioning a Markov process on a terminal event, or
reweighting it by a function of its terminal value, produces a Markov process whose drift
is tilted by $\nabla\log$ of the harmonic function that propagates the terminal weight
backwards. The bridge is the $h$-transform of Brownian motion by $\varphi_1$, started from
$\mu_0$ instead of $m$. The symmetry $\varphi\leftrightarrow\hat\varphi$ is the symmetry of
running time in either direction, and the score of the marginal splits exactly into the two
drifts: $\nabla\log P^\star_t = \nabla\log\varphi_t + \nabla\log\hat\varphi_t$.
</Intuition>

### Iterative proportional fitting

<Definition name="Half bridges and IPF">
For a path measure $Q$ and a target marginal $\nu$, the *half bridges* are
$$
\operatorname*{arg\,min}\{\mathrm{KL}(P\|Q): P_1 = \nu\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname*{arg\,min}\{\mathrm{KL}(P\|Q): P_0 = \nu\}.
$$
*Iterative proportional fitting* (Fortet 1940, Kullback 1968) starts at $P^0 = R$ and
alternates: $P^{2n+1}$ is the terminal half bridge of $P^{2n}$ to $\mu_1$, and $P^{2n+2}$ is
the initial half bridge of $P^{2n+1}$ to $\mu_0$.
</Definition>

<Proposition name="A half bridge keeps the conditional law and swaps the marginal" label="Prop 4">
The terminal half bridge of $Q$ to $\nu$ is $P = \int Q(\cdot\mid X_1 = y)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}y)$,
i.e. the law obtained by drawing $X_1\sim\nu$ and then the rest of the path from $Q$'s
conditional given $X_1$. Its endpoint law is $P_{01}(x,y) = Q_{01}(x,y)\,\nu(y)/Q_1(y)$, the
column rescaling of Sinkhorn. The initial half bridge is the mirror statement.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Chain rule of KL through the map $X_1$:
$\mathrm{KL}(P\|Q) = \mathrm{KL}(P_1\|Q_1) + \int\mathrm{KL}\big(P(\cdot\mid X_1=y)\,\|\,Q(\cdot\mid X_1=y)\big)\nu(\mathrm{d}y)$.
The first term is fixed by the constraint $P_1 = \nu$; the second is minimized, at zero,
by matching conditionals. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

Two consequences, which together are the DSB algorithm (<Cite id="2106.01357" />, Prop. 3
and Alg. 1):

1. *IPF is Sinkhorn on path space.* By induction every iterate has Brownian bridges
   $P^{n,xy} = R^{xy}$ (a half bridge only changes the endpoint law), so $P^n = \int R^{xy}\pi^n$
   with $\pi^n$ the Sinkhorn iterates for the static problem. When Sinkhorn converges,
   $P^n\to P^\star$.
2. *Each half bridge is a time reversal.* $Q(\cdot\mid X_1 = y)$ is the law of $Q$'s path run
   backwards from $y$, so $P^{2n+1}$ is the law of the reverse-time SDE of $P^{2n}$
   initialized at $\mu_1$; its drift is given by the time-reversal formula and involves
   $\nabla\log P^{2n}_t$, which DSB learns by regressing on samples of $P^{2n}$
   (the same mean-matching or score-matching regression as in a diffusion model). Then
   $P^{2n+2}$ is the reversal of that, initialized at $\mu_0$, and so on.

<Warning>
A score-based diffusion model is exactly $P^1$, the first half bridge: the reference is the
noising SDE started at the data, and $P^1$ is its time reversal started at the Gaussian.
$P^1_1 = \mu_1$ exactly, but $P^1_0 = \mu_0$ only if $R_1$ was already Gaussian, which needs
a long horizon. DSB's point is that continuing to $P^2, P^3, \dots$ removes this constraint
and allows short horizons and non-Gaussian $\mu_1$. The price is that the potentials
$\varphi,\hat\varphi$ are never available in closed form, so each iterate is a neural network
trained on the previous one, and errors accumulate; Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching
(<Cite id="2303.16852" />) replaces the KL projections onto marginal constraints by projections
onto Markov and reciprocal classes, which are less sensitive to this.
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="A one-dimensional Gaussian bridge">
Take $d = 1$, $\mu_0 = \mathcal N(0,\alpha^2)$, $\mu_1 = \mathcal N(0,\beta^2)$, reference
$\mathrm{d}X = \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W$ with $X_0\sim m = \mu_0$. Then
$$
R_{01} = \mathcal N\Big(0,\ \Sigma_R\Big),\qquad
\Sigma_R = \begin{pmatrix}\alpha^2 & \alpha^2\\ \alpha^2 & \alpha^2+\sigma^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\Sigma_R = \alpha^2\sigma^2 .
$$

*Step 1: the optimal coupling is Gaussian.* For any $\pi\in\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)$,
$\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|R_{01}) = -H(\pi) - \mathbb{E}_\pi[\log R_{01}]$. Since $\log R_{01}$ is a
quadratic form, the second term depends on $\pi$ only through its covariance
$\Sigma_\pi = \begin{pmatrix}\alpha^2 & c\\ c & \beta^2\end{pmatrix}$, and among all
distributions with that covariance the Gaussian has the largest entropy. So it suffices to
minimize over the single number $c\in(-\alpha\beta,\alpha\beta)$.

*Step 2: KL between the two Gaussians.* Using
$\Sigma_R^{-1} = \frac{1}{\alpha^2\sigma^2}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^2+\sigma^2 & -\alpha^2\\ -\alpha^2 & \alpha^2\end{pmatrix}$,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_R^{-1}\Sigma_\pi) = \frac{\alpha^2(\alpha^2+\sigma^2) - 2\alpha^2c + \alpha^2\beta^2}{\alpha^2\sigma^2}
= \frac{\alpha^2+\beta^2+\sigma^2-2c}{\sigma^2},
$$
so, by the Gaussian KL formula from [Gaussian computations](/concepts/gaussian-computations),
$$
\mathrm{KL}(\pi_c\|R_{01}) = \tfrac12\Big[\frac{\alpha^2+\beta^2-2c}{\sigma^2} - 1 + \log(\alpha^2\sigma^2) - \log(\alpha^2\beta^2-c^2)\Big].
$$
Differentiate in $c$: $\tfrac12\big[-\tfrac{2}{\sigma^2} + \tfrac{2c}{\alpha^2\beta^2-c^2}\big] = 0$
gives $c^2 + \sigma^2c - \alpha^2\beta^2 = 0$, and the root in the admissible range is
$$
c^\star = \frac{-\sigma^2 + \sqrt{\sigma^4 + 4\alpha^2\beta^2}}{2}.
$$
The second derivative is positive, so this is the minimum.

*Limits.* $\sigma\to 0$: $c^\star\to\alpha\beta$, the perfectly correlated coupling
$Y = (\beta/\alpha)X$, which is the $W_2$-optimal map. $\sigma\to\infty$:
$c^\star\approx\alpha^2\beta^2/\sigma^2\to 0$, the independent coupling. Both agree with
Prop 4 on the entropic OT page, with $\varepsilon = \sigma^2$.

*Numbers.* $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 2$, $\sigma = 1$: $c^\star = (-1+\sqrt{17})/2 = 1.5616$,
between the OT value $2$ and the independent value $0$; the correlation is $0.78$.

*Step 3: the marginals.* Under $P^\star$, draw $(X_0,X_1)\sim\pi^\star$ and then a Brownian
bridge: $X_t = (1-t)X_0 + tX_1 + \sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\xi$ with $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$
independent. So $P^\star_t = \mathcal N(0, V_t)$ with
$$
V_t = (1-t)^2\alpha^2 + t^2\beta^2 + 2t(1-t)c^\star + \sigma^2t(1-t).
$$
Check: $V_0 = \alpha^2$, $V_1 = \beta^2$. With the numbers, $V_{1/2} = 0.25 + 1 + 0.7808 + 0.25 = 2.2808$,
slightly above the $W_2$-geodesic value $\big(\tfrac{1+2}{2}\big)^2 = 2.25$ from the
[Benamou–Brenier page](/concepts/benamou-brenier): the bridge spreads a little more because
it is noisy.

*Step 4: the drift.* Since $\pi^\star$ and $r_1$ are both exponentials of quadratics in
$(x,y)$, Thm 2 forces $\hat\varphi_0(x)\varphi_1(y)$ to be one too, with no cross term, so
each potential is an exponential of a quadratic; heat flow preserves this, so
$\log\varphi_t$ is quadratic and the drift $\sigma^2\partial_x\log\varphi_t(x) = k_tx$ is
linear. For $\mathrm{d}X = k_tX\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W$ the variance obeys
$\dot V_t = 2k_tV_t + \sigma^2$ (from [linear SDEs](/concepts/linear-sde-and-ou)), so
$$
k_t = \frac{\dot V_t - \sigma^2}{2V_t},\qquad
\dot V_t = -2(1-t)\alpha^2 + 2t\beta^2 + 2(1-2t)c^\star + \sigma^2(1-2t).
$$
Numbers: $\dot V_0 = -2 + 3.1231 + 1 = 2.1231$, $k_0 = 0.5616$; $\dot V_{1/2} = -1 + 4 = 3$,
$k_{1/2} = 2/(2\cdot 2.2808) = 0.4384$; $\dot V_1 = 8 - 3.1231 - 1 = 3.8769$, $k_1 = 0.3596$.

*Sanity checks.* (i) As $\sigma\to 0$: $c^\star = 2$, $V_t = (1+t)^2$, $k_t = 1/(1+t)$, which
is exactly the Benamou–Brenier velocity $v_t(x) = x/(1+t)$ of the map $T(x) = 2x$. At
$\sigma = 1$ the drift is smaller ($0.56$ vs $1$ at $t = 0$) because diffusion supplies part
of the variance growth for free. (ii) A linear SDE gives
$\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_1) = \alpha^2\exp\!\big(\int_0^1k_t\,\mathrm{d}t\big)$; Simpson's rule
on $(k_0,k_{1/2},k_1)$ gives $\int k\approx(0.5616 + 4(0.4384) + 0.3596)/6 = 0.4458$ and
$e^{0.4458} = 1.562 = c^\star$, so the drift we derived from the variance really does
reproduce the optimal coupling.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **DSB** (<Cite id="2106.01357" />): Prop. 3 there is Prop 4 here plus the time-reversal
  identification; Alg. 1 alternates two score-matching regressions. Their §4 shows that the
  first half bridge is a standard diffusion model.
- **Likelihood training of SB** (<Cite id="2110.11291" />): Thm 3 in forward–backward form;
  the two drifts $\sigma^2\nabla\log\varphi$ and $\sigma^2\nabla\log\hat\varphi$ are the two
  networks, and the identity $\nabla\log P_t = \nabla\log\varphi_t+\nabla\log\hat\varphi_t$
  turns the ELBO of a diffusion model into a training objective for the bridge.
- **DSBM** (<Cite id="2303.16852" />): iterative Markovian fitting, which alternates
  projection onto Markov measures (learn a drift by flow/bridge matching) and onto
  reciprocal measures (re-pair endpoints, keep Brownian bridges); the fixed point is $P^\star$
  by Thm 1 and Thm 3. Schrödinger bridge flow matching and $[\mathrm{SF}]^2\mathrm{M}$ use the
  same decomposition with a Sinkhorn coupling on minibatches.
- **Stochastic control** (<Cite id="1412.4430" />): the bridge minimizes
  $\mathbb{E}\int_0^1\tfrac12\|b_t\|^2\mathrm{d}t$ over controlled SDEs
  $\mathrm{d}X = b\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W$ with the two marginal constraints,
  which is the Girsanov form of KL; in fluid variables it is Benamou–Brenier plus a Fisher
  information term $\tfrac{\sigma^4}{8}\int\!\!\int\|\nabla\log\rho_t\|^2\rho_t$. The
  $\sigma\to 0$ limit is dynamic OT; see [Benamou–Brenier](/concepts/benamou-brenier).
- **Image-to-image** ($\mathrm{I}^2\mathrm{SB}$, Liu et al. 2023): when $\mu_0$ is a point
  mass the potentials are explicit and the bridge is trainable like a diffusion model, which
  is how paired translation problems are handled without IPF.

## Going deeper

Léonard's survey (<Cite id="1308.0215" />) is the reference for everything above in full
generality: existence and uniqueness of $\pi^\star$ under finite-entropy assumptions, the
Schrödinger system, the $h$-transform representation, and the $\sigma\to 0$ Gamma-convergence
to Monge–Kantorovich. Föllmer's 1988 lecture notes are the origin of the KL-on-path-space
formulation. Chen, Georgiou and Pavon (<Cite id="1412.4430" />) develop the stochastic
control and fluid-dynamic viewpoints and treat the linear-Gaussian case, where the potentials
are Gaussian and the Schrödinger system reduces to a matrix Riccati equation; Bunne et al.
(2023) give the closed form for the multivariate Gaussian bridge that generalizes the
one-dimensional computation above.
