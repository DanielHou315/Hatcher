## Why you need this

$\pi_0$ (Black et al. 2024) generates action chunks by integrating a learned velocity field
from Gaussian noise to actions; its training loss is the conditional flow-matching objective
of Lipman et al. 2022 with the optimal-transport path. Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al.
2024) and Rectified Flow (Liu et al. 2022) train the same objective. Minibatch-OT flow
matching (Tong et al. 2023) and the Schrödinger-bridge methods are modifications of the
coupling. To read any of these you need three results: the marginal vector field theorem,
the conditional flow-matching theorem, and the closed form of the velocity for Gaussian
paths. All three are proved below.

## Setup

Time runs from $t = 0$ (noise) to $t = 1$ (data), the flow-matching convention, opposite to
the diffusion convention. Let $q$ be the data distribution on $\mathbb{R}^d$ and
$p_0 = \mathcal N(0,I)$.

A time-dependent vector field $u_t:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ (Lipschitz in $x$,
continuous in $t$) defines a **flow** $\psi_t$ by
$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi_t(x) = u_t(\psi_t(x))$, $\psi_0(x) = x$. The density
$p_t = (\psi_t)_\#p_0$ of $X_t = \psi_t(X_0)$, $X_0\sim p_0$, is the unique solution of the
[continuity equation](/concepts/continuity-equation)
$$
\partial_t p_t + \nabla\!\cdot(p_t\,u_t) = 0 .
$$
We say $u_t$ **generates** the path $(p_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$. Sampling means solving the ODE from
$X_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ to $t = 1$.

<Definition name="Flow matching objective">
Given a target path $p_t$ with $p_1 = q$ and a field $u_t$ generating it, train
$v_\theta(x,t)$ by
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal U[0,1],\;x\sim p_t}\big\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x)\big\|^2 .
$$
This is not computable: we know neither $p_t$ nor $u_t$ for the data we care about.
</Definition>

## The idea

The trick is to build $p_t$ and $u_t$ out of pieces that are simple *conditionally on a
data point*.

<Definition name="Conditional path and conditional field">
For each $x_1\in\mathbb{R}^d$ choose a path $p_t(x|x_1)$ with $p_0(x|x_1) = \mathcal N(0,I)$
and $p_1(\cdot|x_1)$ concentrated near $x_1$, and a field $u_t(x|x_1)$ generating it:
$\partial_t p_t(x|x_1) + \nabla\!\cdot(p_t(x|x_1)\,u_t(x|x_1)) = 0$. The **marginal path**
and **marginal field** are
$$
p_t(x) = \int p_t(x|x_1)\,q(x_1)\,\mathrm{d}x_1,
\qquad
u_t(x) = \int u_t(x|x_1)\,\frac{p_t(x|x_1)\,q(x_1)}{p_t(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x_1 .
$$
The weight $p_t(x|x_1)q(x_1)/p_t(x)$ is the posterior of $x_1$ given $X_t = x$, so
$u_t(x) = \mathbb{E}[u_t(X_t\mid X_1)\mid X_t = x]$: the marginal field is the
[conditional expectation](/concepts/conditional-expectation) of the conditional field.
</Definition>

<Theorem name="The marginal field generates the marginal path (Lipman et al., Thm 1)" label="Thm 1">
Assume $q$ has finite first moment, $p_t(x|x_1)>0$, and $p_t(x|x_1)$, $u_t(x|x_1)$ are
smooth enough that $\partial_t$ and $\nabla\!\cdot$ can be exchanged with $\int\cdot\,q(x_1)\mathrm{d}x_1$
(dominated convergence; true for the Gaussian paths below). Then
$\partial_t p_t + \nabla\!\cdot(p_t u_t) = 0$, with $p_1 = q$ when $p_1(\cdot|x_1) = \delta_{x_1}$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Differentiate the marginal in $t$ under the integral and use the conditional continuity
equation:
$$
\partial_t p_t(x) = \int\partial_t p_t(x|x_1)\,q(x_1)\,\mathrm{d}x_1
= -\int\nabla\!\cdot\big(u_t(x|x_1)\,p_t(x|x_1)\big)\,q(x_1)\,\mathrm{d}x_1 .
$$
Pull the divergence (in $x$) out of the integral (in $x_1$):
$$
= -\nabla\!\cdot\int u_t(x|x_1)\,p_t(x|x_1)\,q(x_1)\,\mathrm{d}x_1
= -\nabla\!\cdot\big(u_t(x)\,p_t(x)\big),
$$
where the last step is the definition of $u_t$ multiplied through by $p_t(x)$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Theorem name="Conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., Thm 2)" label="Thm 2">
Define
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal U[0,1],\;x_1\sim q,\;x\sim p_t(\cdot|x_1)}\big\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x|x_1)\big\|^2 .
$$
If $\mathbb{E}\|u_t(X_t|X_1)\|^2<\infty$, then $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}} = \mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}} + C$
with $C$ independent of $\theta$; in particular $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}} = \nabla_\theta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Expand both squares at fixed $t$. The terms $\mathbb{E}\|v_\theta(x,t)\|^2$ agree because
the marginal of $x$ under $q(x_1)p_t(x|x_1)$ is $p_t(x)$. The terms without $\theta$ go
into $C$. The cross terms:
$$
\mathbb{E}_{x_1,\,x}\big\langle v_\theta(x,t),u_t(x|x_1)\big\rangle
= \int\Big\langle v_\theta(x,t),\int u_t(x|x_1)\,p_t(x|x_1)\,q(x_1)\,\mathrm{d}x_1\Big\rangle\mathrm{d}x
= \int\big\langle v_\theta(x,t),u_t(x)\,p_t(x)\big\rangle\mathrm{d}x
= \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_t}\big\langle v_\theta(x,t),u_t(x)\big\rangle ,
$$
using Fubini and the definition of $u_t$. Integrate over $t$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Intuition>
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}$ is least-squares regression of a noisy target
$u_t(x|x_1)$ on the input $x$. The $L^2$-optimal regressor is the conditional mean, which is
$u_t(x)$ by definition. Exactly the same logic makes denoising score matching work
(compare Cor 3 on the [score matching](/concepts/score-matching) page).
</Intuition>

<Theorem name="Gaussian conditional paths (Lipman et al., Thm 3)" label="Thm 3">
Let $p_t(x|x_1) = \mathcal N\big(x;\,\mu_t(x_1),\,\sigma_t(x_1)^2 I\big)$ with $\mu_t,\sigma_t$
$C^1$ in $t$, $\sigma_t>0$ for $t<1$, $\mu_0 = 0$, $\sigma_0 = 1$. The map
$\psi_t(x_0|x_1) = \sigma_t(x_1)\,x_0 + \mu_t(x_1)$ pushes $\mathcal N(0,I)$ to $p_t(\cdot|x_1)$,
and the field generating the path is
$$
u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{\sigma_t'(x_1)}{\sigma_t(x_1)}\big(x - \mu_t(x_1)\big) + \mu_t'(x_1) .
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
An affine image of $\mathcal N(0,I)$ is $\mathcal N(\mu_t,\sigma_t^2I)$
([affine maps of Gaussians](/concepts/gaussian-computations)). The flow generated by
$u_t(\cdot|x_1)$ must satisfy $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi_t(x_0) = u_t(\psi_t(x_0)|x_1)$.
Differentiate: $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi_t(x_0) = \sigma_t'x_0 + \mu_t'$. Solve
$x = \sigma_tx_0 + \mu_t$ for $x_0 = (x-\mu_t)/\sigma_t$ and substitute. Since the
push-forward of $p_0$ by this flow is $p_t(\cdot|x_1)$ for every $t$, the field generates
the path (uniqueness for the continuity equation). $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

**Two paths.**

- *Optimal-transport (OT) path:* $\mu_t = t\,x_1$, $\sigma_t = 1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t$. Then
  $$
  u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})\,x}{1-(1-\sigma_{\min})\,t},
  \qquad
  \psi_t(x_0|x_1) = \big(1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t\big)x_0 + t\,x_1 .
  $$
  Along a trajectory $x = \psi_t(x_0|x_1)$ the field is $u = x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})x_0$,
  constant in $t$: each conditional flow moves particles in straight lines at constant
  speed. With $\sigma_{\min}\to 0$ the regression target is simply $x_1 - x_0$, the form
  used by Rectified Flow, SD3 and $\pi_0$. The name comes from
  [Benamou–Brenier](/concepts/benamou-brenier): $x_0\mapsto\sigma_{\min}x_0 + x_1$ is the
  gradient of the convex function $\tfrac12\sigma_{\min}\|x\|^2+\langle x_1,x\rangle$, hence the
  $W_2$-optimal map from $\mathcal N(0,I)$ to $\mathcal N(x_1,\sigma_{\min}^2I)$, and
  $\psi_t$ is its displacement interpolation. The *marginal* flow is not $W_2$-optimal in
  general; minibatch-OT CFM (Tong et al. 2023) changes the coupling of $(x_0,x_1)$ to move
  toward that.
- *Diffusion (VP) path:* $\mu_t = \alpha_{1-t}x_1$, $\sigma_t = \sqrt{1-\alpha_{1-t}^2}$ with
  $\alpha_s = e^{-\frac12\int_0^s\beta}$; this is the DDPM perturbation kernel with the
  clock reversed. Thm 3 gives the velocity in closed form; its trajectories are curved.

<Proposition name="Velocity and score" label="Prop 4">
For a conditional path $p_t(x|x_1) = \mathcal N(a_tx_1,\sigma_t^2I)$ with $a_t\ne0$, the
marginal field and the marginal score are related pointwise by
$$
u_t(x) = \frac{a_t'}{a_t}\,x + \Big(\sigma_t^2\frac{a_t'}{a_t} - \sigma_t\sigma_t'\Big)\nabla\log p_t(x) .
$$
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Write $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[X_1\mid X_t = x]$. Thm 3 gives
$u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{\sigma_t'}{\sigma_t}(x - a_tx_1) + a_t'x_1$, affine in $x_1$, so taking
the conditional expectation (the definition of $u_t$) gives
$u_t(x) = \frac{\sigma_t'}{\sigma_t}(x-a_tm(x)) + a_t'm(x)$. Tweedie's formula
(affine case, [score matching](/concepts/score-matching) Thm 4) gives
$a_tm(x) = x + \sigma_t^2\nabla\log p_t(x)$. Substitute:
$$
u_t(x) = \frac{\sigma_t'}{\sigma_t}\big(-\sigma_t^2\nabla\log p_t\big) + \frac{a_t'}{a_t}\big(x+\sigma_t^2\nabla\log p_t\big),
$$
and collect terms. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

So a flow-matching network and a score network carry the same information at every $t$; a
velocity is a score plus a known linear term. For $a_t = 1$ the formula reads
$u_t = -\sigma_t\sigma_t'\nabla\log p_t$, which is the probability-flow ODE of the VE-SDE
in the noise-to-data clock ([diffusion SDE framework](/concepts/diffusion-sde-framework),
Thm 3).

<Warning>
Conditional trajectories are straight, but the marginal ODE's trajectories generally are
not: two conditional straight lines $\psi_t(x_0|x_1)$ and $\psi_t(x_0'|x_1')$ can cross,
while integral curves of a Lipschitz field $u_t$ never do. Averaging (Thm 1) bends them.
"Straight" in Rectified Flow refers to the coupling after reflow, not to the first-round
marginal flow. Also, with $\sigma_{\min} = 0$ the conditional target $u_t(x|x_1) = (x_1-x)/(1-t)$
is unbounded as $t\to 1$ for $x$ off the conditional path, but the regression target on
the path, $x_1 - x_0$, is fine; the finite-second-moment hypothesis of Thm 2 holds.
</Warning>

## Worked example by hand

<Example title="Two data points in one dimension: the CFM target and the marginal field">
Data: $q = \tfrac12\delta_{-1}+\tfrac12\delta_{+1}$. OT path with $\sigma_{\min} = 0$, so
$p_t(x|x_1) = \mathcal N(tx_1,(1-t)^2)$ and $u_t(x|x_1) = (x_1-x)/(1-t)$.

*A training sample.* Draw $x_0 = -0.5$, $x_1 = +1$, $t = \tfrac12$. The input is
$x = \psi_{1/2}(x_0|x_1) = 0.5\cdot(-0.5) + 0.5\cdot1 = 0.25$ and the target is
$u = x_1 - x_0 = 1.5$; equivalently $(x_1-x)/(1-t) = 0.75/0.5 = 1.5$. Had the data point
been $x_1 = -1$ with the same $x = 0.25$, the target would be $(-1-0.25)/0.5 = -2.5$. The
network at $(x,t) = (0.25,\tfrac12)$ is pulled toward $1.5$ by one kind of sample and
toward $-2.5$ by the other.

*The marginal field.* By Thm 1 the field to learn is the posterior average of these two
targets. The marginal is the mixture $p_t = \tfrac12\mathcal N(-t,(1-t)^2)+\tfrac12\mathcal N(t,(1-t)^2)$
and the posterior weight of $x_1 = +1$ given $x$ is
$$
w_+(x) = \frac{e^{-(x-t)^2/2(1-t)^2}}{e^{-(x-t)^2/2(1-t)^2}+e^{-(x+t)^2/2(1-t)^2}}
= \frac{1}{1+e^{-2xt/(1-t)^2}},
$$
since $(x+t)^2-(x-t)^2 = 4xt$. Then
$\mathbb{E}[X_1\mid X_t = x] = w_+ - w_- = \tanh\!\big(xt/(1-t)^2\big)$ and
$$
u_t(x) = \frac{\tanh\!\big(xt/(1-t)^2\big) - x}{1-t}.
$$
At $(x,t) = (0.25,\tfrac12)$: $(1-t)^2 = 0.25$, $xt/(1-t)^2 = 0.125/0.25 = 0.5$,
$\tanh(0.5) = 0.4621$, so $u_{1/2}(0.25) = (0.4621-0.25)/0.5 = 0.4242$. As a weighted
average of the two conditional targets: $w_+ = 1/(1+e^{-1}) = 0.7311$, $w_- = 0.2689$, and
$0.7311\cdot1.5 - 0.2689\cdot2.5 = 1.0966 - 0.6723 = 0.4243$. Agreement to rounding.

*Cross-check through the score (Prop 4).* Here $a_t = t$, $\sigma_t = 1-t$, so at $t=\tfrac12$:
$a'/a = 2$ and $\sigma^2a'/a - \sigma\sigma' = 0.25\cdot2 + 0.5 = 1$. The score of the
symmetric mixture with means $\pm m$, variance $v$ is $-x/v + (m/v)\tanh(mx/v)$; with
$m = 0.5$, $v = 0.25$, $x = 0.25$: $-1 + 2\tanh(0.5) = -1 + 0.9242 = -0.0758$. Then
$u = 2\cdot0.25 + 1\cdot(-0.0758) = 0.4242$. Same number by an independent route.

*Sanity checks.* At $t = 0$, $u_0(x) = \tanh(0) - x = -x$: with no information about
which data point will be drawn, every particle first drifts toward the data mean $0$. As
$t\to1$ with $x$ near $+t$, $\tanh\to1$ and $u\to(1-x)/(1-t)$, the pure conditional field
toward $+1$. The field is odd, $u_t(-x) = -u_t(x)$, matching the symmetry of $q$. And the
particle from $x_0 = -0.5$ that we started with is at $0.25$ at $t = \tfrac12$ with marginal
velocity $0.42$, while its conditional velocity was $1.5$: the marginal flow is slower there
because a quarter of the posterior mass still points to $-1$.
</Example>

## Where it shows up

- **Flow Matching** <Cite id="2210.02747" />: Thms 1–3 are their Theorems 1–3; the CFM
  loss is their eq. (9); the OT path is Section 4.2 and the diffusion path Section 4.1.
  The [paper page](/papers/flow-matching-lipman-2022) decomposes it.
- **Rectified Flow** <Cite id="2209.03003" />: the same objective with target $x_1-x_0$
  and an arbitrary coupling of $(x_0,x_1)$; "reflow" re-couples using the learned flow to
  straighten marginal trajectories (see the Warning).
- **$\pi_0$** <Cite id="2410.24164" />: the action expert is trained with the OT
  conditional path on action chunks, conditioned on the VLM's observation embedding, and
  samples by Euler-integrating the learned field for about ten steps. The only change from
  Thm 2 is the distribution of $t$, which is skewed toward noisier times.
- **SD3** <Cite id="2403.03206" />: the same objective with a logit-normal distribution
  over $t$; the paper's ablation compares the OT path against the VP path of Thm 3.
- **Minibatch OT CFM** <Cite id="2302.00482" />: replaces the independent coupling
  $p_0\otimes q$ by an entropic-OT plan on each minibatch
  ([Sinkhorn](/concepts/entropic-ot-and-sinkhorn)); Thms 1–2 still apply because they
  never used independence of $x_0$ and $x_1$, only that $x\sim p_t(\cdot|x_1)$.
- **Schrödinger bridges** ([page](/concepts/schrodinger-bridge)): the stochastic analogue,
  in which the conditional path is a Brownian bridge and the coupling is the entropic-OT
  plan; the flow-matching limit is the zero-noise case.
- **Diffusion policies with flow matching** (e.g. $\pi_0$, and flow-based variants of
  Diffusion Policy): Prop 4 is why a velocity network can be fine-tuned with the same
  guidance and RL machinery as a score network.

its trajectories $\psi_t(x_0|x_1)$ are not straight lines. Compare with the OT path at the

</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="The kinetic energy of the conditional flow is |x_1 - (1 - sigma_min) x_0|^2 for every particle; compare with the W_2 formula between Gaussians.">
Using [Benamou–Brenier](/concepts/benamou-brenier), show that the conditional OT path
attains $W_2^2\big(\mathcal N(0,I),\mathcal N(x_1,\sigma_{\min}^2I)\big) = \|x_1\|^2 + d(1-\sigma_{\min})^2$,
and hence that $\psi_t(\cdot|x_1)$ is the $W_2$ geodesic.
</Exercise>

<Exercise hint="Write both objectives' cross terms and use Tweedie in the affine form; the weighting is (sigma^2 a'/a - sigma sigma')^2.">
Show that for a Gaussian conditional path the CFM loss equals a denoising score matching
loss with a specific time weighting, and compute that weighting for the OT path with
$\sigma_{\min} = 0$. Where does it vanish, and what does that mean for the score there?
</Exercise>

## Going deeper

Lipman et al. 2022 is short and readable once Thms 1–3 are in hand; Appendix A has the
regularity conditions for Thm 1. Tong et al. 2023 give the cleanest general statement
(arbitrary couplings) and connect to Schrödinger bridges. Liu, Gong and Liu 2022 explain
reflow and why straight marginal flows allow one-step sampling. For the $W_2$ geometry
behind the OT path read [Benamou–Brenier](/concepts/benamou-brenier) and then
[Wasserstein distance](/concepts/wasserstein-distance).
